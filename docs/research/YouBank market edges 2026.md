# Win the decision record, not search

YouBank's biggest openings in November 2026 are in **scoring and proving a fund's own decisions**, not in finding or summarising information. Search and summaries are now bundled into Bloomberg (ASKB went generally available on 15 Oct 2026), FactSet, and the finance editions of Claude and ChatGPT, while research budgets are flat (**+0.4% in H1 2026**) and AI tooling grows only by taking share from brokers ([Substantive Research](https://substantiveresearch.com/insights-and-press/buy-side-research-spend-continues-to-flatline-despite-new-ai-driven-requirements/)). Nobody in the competitor set checks whether a held position's thesis still stands or measures a PM's decision quality before the trade. Allocators and the SEC now want evidence that AI use is governed, and **only 17% of funds say AI improved investment performance** ([Neudata via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/alternative-data-budget-confidence-hits-3-year-high-while-ai-returns-remain-efficiency-led--neudatas-2026-industry-report-302881293.html)). The ranked plan is: (1) a decision-attribution engine that starts with "did your exits beat a random sale?"; (2) a proof-of-process tear sheet with a verification link, for emerging managers who are raising and the allocators who vet them; (3) measuring the uplift AI gives each forecaster. Then come (4) a verified-figures layer on everything YouBank drafts, (5) the MCP server as a process layer listed in the Claude and ChatGPT finance directories, (6) nightly re-underwriting of every thesis, (7) committee noise audits, and two speculative bets: (8) public verified track records and (9) event-contract kill criteria. Do not build LLM stock picks, persona simulators sold as prediction, debate-to-consensus committees, or more terminal and chat-over-filings breadth. The evidence argues against the first three, and incumbents and frontier labs already own the last. One caveat applies throughout: the research notes behind this report were gathered from search-result summaries because primary pages were blocked, and most vendor prices are third-party estimates.

## Search is being bundled and decision quality is unclaimed

The money for AI research tools comes out of budgets that already exist. Substantive Research's October 2026 survey of 50 managers with about $20T AUM found AI and analytics tooling up **40% in a year to about 7% of research budgets, "mostly at the expense of brokers and independent providers"** ([FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/buy-side-research-budgets-flatline-as-ai-spending-rises-40)). A new product therefore has to replace a line item: an AlphaSense seat (about $10k–$20k a year), a Hebbia seat (about $3k for Lite, $10k for full) or a broker payment ([Allmind](https://allmind.ai/research/alphasense-pricing-2026); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/06c453cc-9871-47c4-bc57-83b47d85c44c/)). Incumbents are folding AI into seats people already pay for. About a third of the Terminal's 375,000 users had ASKB by April 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/press/hedge-fund-alpha-inside-bloombergs-askb-how-the-terminal-is-rethinking-agentic-ai/)). OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services on 10 Sep 2026 with Morgan Stanley and Evercore as design partners ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-ships-chatgpt-for-financial-services-with-morgan-stanley-and-evercore-as)). Anthropic ships ten finance agent templates and connectors for FactSet, Morningstar, PitchBook and Daloopa ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-financial-services)).

Every one of these competes on finding information. The fund's own decision record sits with notes systems: AlphaSense integrated with Bipsync in July 2026 with Bipsync as the "system-of-record" for internal knowledge, and Bipsync has a Decision content type but no kill criteria, calibration or bias analytics ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/14/3326983/0/en/bipsync-and-alphasense-integrate-to-put-clients-institutional-knowledge-inside-market-intelligence-workflows.html); [Bipsync](https://bipsync.com/blog/bipsync-launches-toro-an-ai-research-assistant-that-puts-institutional-knowledge-at-investors-fingertips/)). Essentia Analytics comes closest on decision quality, covering about $430B, but it analyses long-only trades after the fact and runs no thesis workflow ([Essentia](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/26q2-ebab-insights/)). The large multi-manager funds build this in-house: Citadel's equities tool builds portfolio-specific reading lists and risk highlights ([Inflxd](https://media.inflxd.com/article/citadel-balyasny-push-proprietary-ai-deeper-into-analyst-and-pm-)). Funds outside the large platforms have no equivalent to buy.

Demand comes from three directions at once. Adoption is almost universal, but returns show up as efficiency: **66% of funds use AI mainly for productivity versus 31% for strategy** ([Neudata](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alternative-data-budget-confidence-hits-3-year-high-while-ai-returns-remain-efficiency-led--neudatas-2026-industry-report-302881291.html)). The SEC's FY2026 exam priorities test the accuracy of AI claims and the governance, controls and human oversight of AI use, and they prioritise newly registered advisers ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/11/2026-sec-division-of-examinations-priorities); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/alerts-privateequity-pif-2026-sec-exam-priorities-for-registered-investment-advisers)). There is no AI rule to comply with, because the predictive-analytics proposal was withdrawn in June 2025 ([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals)). What exams test is evidence that the firm followed its own policy. Allocators have moved from "do you use AI?" to "show us the policy, the oversight and the audit trail" ([DiligenceVault](https://diligencevault.com/gaim-ops-cayman-2026-key-takeaways/)).

YouBank already owns most of the raw material. The Book checks kill criteria against SEC XBRL every weekday without AI (`src/lib/book/watch.ts`). Forecasts are scored by Brier with Platt recalibration, and anti-gaming rules apply: a forecast cannot be withdrawn or backdated, the minimum horizon is 30 days, and no forecast is taken on a broken thesis (`src/lib/book/forecast.ts`). The decision journal is written by the server so it cannot be backfilled (`src/lib/book/decisions.ts`). Process health tracks seven habits (`src/lib/book/process.ts`). The AI committee writes bull and bear briefs independently and pools several chairs in log-odds (`src/lib/book/committee.ts`). The hash-chained ledger has daily email anchoring, a governance report and a DDQ AI section answered from the record (`src/lib/ledger/`). The LP letter has a Marketing Rule and AI-washing pass (`src/lib/book/letter.ts`). The MCP server exposes Book tools and an MCP Apps proposal card (`src/lib/mcp/server.ts`, `apps.ts`). Edge also has Calibrated Claims (E2), Call Desk evasion scoring (E5) and a budgeted Thesis Agent runtime (E7) (`docs/edge-next.md`), which the Book does not use yet. Most opportunities below reuse these pieces rather than start from nothing.

### 1. Show PMs whether their exits beat a coin flip

The best-evidenced finding in the decision-science literature is one that PMs rarely get to see for themselves: **institutional managers buy well but sell badly, underperforming even random-selling strategies** (Akepanidtaworn, Di Mascio, Imas and Schmidt, *Journal of Finance* 2023, winner of the 2024 Dimensional Fund Advisors Prize) ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w29076); [AFA](https://afajof.org/news/journal-of-finance-webinar-2024-dimensional-fund-advisors-prize)). Vendor data says the same thing in 2026: in Q2, **81.2% of 207 portfolios destroyed value when scaling out** ([Essentia](https://www.essentia-analytics.com/26q2-ebab-insights/)). Ideas also go stale. Essentia's study of about 10,000 position episodes found the average idea held 275 business days, against about 133 for the top-alpha quartile, and estimated about 1.2% a year lost to over-attachment ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1hxzwxndzj7rc/Active-Equity-Managers-Actually-Do-Generate-Alpha-Here-s-How-They-Squander-It)). Inalytics and Essentia sell versions of this to pension funds as consulting-style reports ([top1000funds](https://www.top1000funds.com/organisational-design/working-hard-for-the-money-funds-managers-need-to-self-examine/)). YouBank can do something none of them do: join every sell to the thesis state and the stated reason at the time it was made. The question then changes from "you sell badly" to "you sell badly when the price moved sharply and no kill criterion fired".

### 3. Measure the uplift AI gives each forecaster

Every fund can say it uses AI. Almost none can show it made their judgements better, and that is the question budgets will turn on. The forecasting literature gives YouBank a way to measure it. In an RCT with 991 participants, **frontier LLM assistants improved human forecasting accuracy by 24–28%**, and even a deliberately biased assistant helped ([Schoenegger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07862)). Under an hour of probabilistic-reasoning training improved Brier scores by **6–11%** across a four-year tournament ([Chang et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/judgdm/v11y2016i5p509-526_8.html)). Forecasters who update in small, frequent steps are more accurate, which gives an early skill signal months before Brier scores settle ([Atanasov et al.](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/1-s2.0-S0749597819300949-main.pdf)). That matters when theses resolve slowly. Finance professionals are badly overprecise: CFOs' 80% intervals for S&P 500 returns held the outcome **only about 36% of the time** ([Ben-David, Graham and Harvey](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16215.pdf)).

| | |
|---|---|
| **What to build** | (a) *Before-AI and after-AI forecasts*: on forecast entry, the PM records their own probability first, stored as source "You, before AI". They can then open a forecast assistant (superforecaster-style prompt, base rate from `baserate.ts`, the committee's pooled number) before saving the final one. Both are scored, and calibration shows "AI uplift" as a Brier difference per person and team over time. (b) *Updating profile*, from the forecast history `supersede` already keeps: update frequency, median step, share of jumps over 20 points, and "stuck" forecasts that were not revised despite a near-break or a move beyond the volatility base rate. (c) *Granularity check*: the share of forecasts on multiples of 10, and whether rounding the PM's forecasts to 10s would hurt their Brier score. If it would not, their precision carries no information. (d) *Interval forecasts*: an 80% range for next-quarter revenue growth or margin, resolved automatically from XBRL, with the hit rate against 80% and a suggested widening factor. (e) *Fitted extremizing* of the committee pool: `committee.ts` pools in log-odds without extremizing, so fit the parameter on the fund's own resolved history and shrink it toward none, because extremizing a wrong-sided average hurts ([Baron et al.](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2015---two-reasons-to-make-aggregated-probability-forecasts_1.pdf); [Satopää](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2148v2)). (f) An optional under-one-hour training module, with before and after Brier scores. |
| **Who pays, how much** | Every plan. On Pro it is the hook that makes the forecast habit sticky. On Deal Team and Enterprise it is the AI-ROI evidence a CIO shows the partners and allocators, and it feeds the tear sheet in #2. |
| **Why now** | 56% of funds report a tangible AI return, but mostly efficiency; 17% report performance ([Neudata](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/alternative-data-budget-confidence-hits-3-year-high-while-ai-returns-remain-efficiency-led--neudatas-2026-industry-report-302881293.html)). This feature produces the missing number. |
| **Evidence (strength)** | **Strong** for the methods: replicated Good Judgment Project findings, a randomised trial, and a top-journal overprecision study. There is no finance-specific replication of the updating or LLM-uplift results, so the claim must stay "measure your uplift", never "gain 25%". |
| **Fit and reuse** | `forecast.ts` (sources already separate the PM from the AI committee), `baserate.ts`, `committee.ts`, the calibration item in `process.ts`, and XBRL facts for interval resolution. |
| **Effort** | S for (a)–(c) and (e); M for interval forecasts, which need a new resolution path beside the binary kill-criterion one. |
| **Risks** | Brier needs dozens of resolved forecasts with a 30-day minimum horizon, so uplift numbers take months, and the updating profile fills that gap. Showing the AI number before the PM commits would anchor them, so the order is enforced. |
| **First shippable slice** | The before/after pair on forecast entry, a calibration split by "own" vs "after AI", and the granularity statistic in process health. |

### 4. Put a verified-figures check on everything YouBank drafts

The most common complaint about AI research tools is a confident wrong number when the data cannot support the answer ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-funds-deploy-ai-for-speed-gains-in-research/)). Grounded pipelines get finance hallucinations into single digits, **4.9% end to end on FinanceBench, but computational claims still fail 28.4% of the time** ([FinGround](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23588v1)). Accuracy also drops 14–19% when tasks cross companies or years ([Fin-RATE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07294)). Vendors already warn about hallucinated DDQ answers ([GovernGPT](https://www.governgpt.com/blog/fund-manager-ai-hallucination-ddq)), and SEC examiners test whether AI claims are accurate. YouBank holds structured ground truth (XBRL, prices, Form 4) and an audit ledger, so it can do what chat-over-filings tools cannot: check every number deterministically and keep the receipt.

### 5. Sell the MCP server as a process layer inside Claude and ChatGPT

Funds already work inside general assistants: **77% of buy-side firms have organisation-wide generative AI platforms** ([Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/substantive-research-buy-side-ai-adoption-hindered-by-broker-research-licensing-2026716/)), and Walleye says all its employees use Claude Code ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-financial-services)). The connectors in those assistants carry public and licensed data (FactSet, S&P, LSEG, Morningstar, Daloopa), not the fund's own theses, journal and forecasts ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/financial-services)). MCP Apps, launched on 26 Jan 2026, let a server render interactive UI inside Claude, ChatGPT and VS Code ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-01-26-mcp-apps/)). With the official MCP registry reportedly past 30,000 servers ([dev.to](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc)), a generic listing gets little discovery. The curated finance directories are where a listing matters. An MCP server alone is table stakes. The edge comes from what it exposes: the fund's own decision state, as actions that complement the data vendors rather than duplicate them.

### 6. Re-underwrite every thesis overnight

Nobody automatically checks each held position's thesis against new filings, insider activity and calls. Research tools answer questions you ask, and the platforms that do this monitoring built it themselves. Agents can now run long enough for the job: METR reports 50% time horizons of **16+ hours and 80% horizons of about 3 hours** for top early-2026 models ([Wikipedia: METR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR)). Frontier models answer about two-thirds of real analyst tasks on the Vals Finance Agent benchmark (Claude Opus 5.5 at **67.85%**). Costs per task range from $8.91 down to $0.18 at near-identical accuracy, and only about a third of modelling tasks are solved ([Vals v2.1](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2-1); [Vals v2](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2)). The design consequence: run agents on the 3-hour reliable horizon, route most steps to cheap models, and present output as a sourced diff to verify, not a finished verdict.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What to build** | An opt-in nightly pass on top of `watch.ts`, which stays deterministic and AI-free. It runs on Edge's E7 agent runtime, which is budgeted, uses typed tools only, and logs every step. For each position with new information it pulls the filing diff (the `what_changed` logic), new Form 4s and 8-K items, and Call Desk (E5) evasion flags on Q&A touching the thesis's pillars. It then produces (a) a memo diff, "what changed for this thesis", with every claim through E2; (b) an AI probability that each kill criterion fires before the forecast date, scored as an AI source alongside the committee; and (c) a "needs you" item when a pillar weakened but no criterion failed, which is a criteria blind spot that links into the `pre_mortem` prompt. |
| **Who pays, how much** | Deal Team for the whole book; Pro for one position, matching E7's free tier. Model spend counts against the owner's AI allowance with a per-position budget. Edge's thesis ticks cost about $0.35 per thesis-month (`docs/pricing.md`), which sets the scale. |
| **Why now** | Agent horizons crossed the overnight threshold this year. Earnings volume keeps rising: over 65% of S&P 500 Q2 2026 calls cited AI ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-preview-q2-2026)). |
| **Evidence (strength)** | **Moderate**: capability evidence is strong; demand is inferred from the gap and from multi-manager funds building it in-house, not from a survey. Earnings-call voice and evasion signals are short-horizon only ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890838926001642)). |
| **Fit and reuse** | E7, E5 and E2 from Edge; `watch.ts` and `alerts.ts` for delivery; forecast sources from `forecast.ts`. |
| **Effort** | M–L. |
| **Risks** | Cost for a 40-position book; alert fatigue; hallucinated "changes" (hence E2 on every claim); MNPI and compliance concerns, which is why E7 avoids open-web browsing. |
| **First shippable slice** | Earnings-triggered only: when a held name files a 10-Q or 10-K, run the filing diff, re-check criteria and compute the AI kill probability once, delivered as a sourced Book alert. |

### 7. Audit committee noise and give AI chairs different evidence

Noise, meaning disagreement among professionals looking at the same case, is large and underestimated. In one audit, underwriters' premiums for identical cases differed by a **median of about 55%, against the 10% executives expected** ([Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/a-conversation-with-daniel-kahneman-about-noise/)). Structured devil's advocacy beat expert-only decisions in a meta-analysis ([Schwenk 1990](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/999233521)), and an interactive LLM devil's advocate improved groups' appropriate reliance on AI in a randomised experiment ([Chiang et al. 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10524816)). YouBank's committee already avoids the main failure mode of debating agents, because briefs are written independently. But research on multi-agent forecasting says debate on identical input adds no expected accuracy. The benefit comes from agents holding **different evidence**: on 375 questions, debate with asymmetric evidence scored Brier 0.202 against 0.245 for an ensemble ([arXiv 2607.01661](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01661v1)). In `committee.ts`, both chairs currently receive the same evidence lines.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What to build** | (a) *Evidence-asymmetric chairs*: the bull brief gets management's language and filing changes; the bear brief gets insider selling, net-debt trends, valuation and short-relevant data. Each chair's forecast is stored with its own source so its Brier is tracked. (b) *Committee noise audit for teams*: using team book sharing, each member scores the same thesis packet blind (probability the thesis is right, expected return, size, kill level) before seeing anyone else. Timestamps enforce independence. Report dispersion split into level noise (always more bullish) and pattern noise, and re-run the same packet weeks later to measure occasion noise. (c) *Devil's advocate against consensus*: when the PM and committee agree, an interactive agent argues the other side and answers the PM's specific points. Overrides are logged so "appropriate reliance" can be scored. (d) A *herding* process-health item: dispersion before discussion and convergence after. (e) A periodic *bias audit* of the AI committee with planted contradictory evidence, because LLMs given a little supporting evidence become reluctant to change their view ([arXiv 2507.20957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20957v1)). This answers the allocator question "how do I know your AI isn't just agreeing with the PM?" |
| **Who pays, how much** | Multi-PM teams; family offices with $500M–$5B and small investment staffs, where 40% describe AI adoption as early-stage and 19% have none ([Citi via WorkWise](https://workwisesolutions.org/guides/family-office-ai-statistics.html)); and RIAs that run a formal IC. Deal Team (three-seat minimum) and Enterprise. Family-office contracts plausibly reach $10k+ a year, but that is unvalidated. |
| **Why now** | 65% of family offices intend to prioritise AI ([J.P. Morgan](https://privatebank.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm-pb-aem/global/en/documents/2026-family-office-report/jp-morgan-private-bank-2026-global-family-office-report.pdf)), and governance reporting to the family or board is their pain point. |
| **Evidence (strength)** | **Moderate**: noise audits are consultancy case studies, the devil's-advocacy meta-analysis is older lab work, the LLM advocate rests on one RCT, and the multi-agent results are preprints plus NeurIPS theory ([Debate or Vote](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)). No peer-reviewed noise audit of investment committees exists. |
| **Fit and reuse** | `committee.ts` (independent briefs and pooled chairs), `store.ts` team sharing, forecast sources. |
| **Effort** | S for the chairs; M for the team noise audit. |
| **Risks** | Teams resist the extra step; three-person committees give noisy dispersion statistics. |
| **First shippable slice** | Evidence-asymmetric chairs with per-chair Brier on the calibration page. This is a configuration change inside `committee.ts`. |

## The evidence argues against six builds

**Do not build LLM stock picks or AI trading signals.** Apparent LLM forecasting power on stocks "collapses sharply" after a model's training cutoff ([Gao, Jiang and Yan](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v2)). GPT-4's price-forecast errors are about 16% lower before its cutoff than after, which is evidence of leakage in naive backtests ([Liang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6772819.pdf?abstractid=6772819&mirid=1&type=2)). Six frontier models trading real money on Kalshi lost between **16.0% and 30.8%** over 57 days ([Prediction Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1)). LLM return forecasts behave like extrapolative retail investors ([AEA 2026](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/zNrQ4Yn6)). YouBank's edge is the opposite stance: label any signal test "post-model-cutoff only" and keep the claim inside the ledger. The same evidence counsels caution on marketing AI-driven modes in Algo trading.

**Do not sell persona simulation as prediction.** LLM investor personas built from the same corpus pick the same media-prominent stocks whatever their framing ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/do-llm-crowds-produce-investment-signals-an-empirical-test/?a=6080)), and synthetic respondents audit as "plausible but not valid" ([arXiv 2608.14606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14606)). "Argue as a short-seller would" is fine as a labelled red team, which the `red_team` and `pre_mortem` prompts already do. "How holders will react" is not.

**Do not let committee agents debate to consensus.** Debate adds nothing over voting in theory ([NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Under a fixed budget, independent sampling matches or beats agentic approaches ([arXiv 2605.08478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08478)), and trading debates collapse through sycophantic convergence ([arXiv 2609.29701](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29701)). The current design is right. Extend it with different evidence per chair, not with more rounds of talk.

**Do not chase segments that platforms own.** For FP&A, Microsoft's Finance Agent and Excel Agent Mode own the surface ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/he-il/copilot/release-plan/2026wave1/finance-agents)), so serve it, if at all, as MCP tools inside Excel. The sell-side is lab-native, and Rogo reports over $50M ARR ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/rogo-arr-triples-50m-surpasses-hebbia/)). PE portfolio-monitoring data belongs to Chronograph, which raised $140M+ ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/06/16/chronograph-secures-140m-from-sixth-street-growth/)); YouBank's PE angle is the IC record, not the data layer. Communications archiving belongs to Smarsh and Global Relay, which should be export targets. Corporate IR showed no demand evidence.

**Do not use journal tone as an alpha signal.** In shareholder letters, positive tone predicts more risk-taking and style bets, not better performance ([Hillert et al., *Management Science* 2025](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:71:y:2025:i:5:p:4453-4473)). Language analytics on the journal is defensible only as a coaching flag for building overconfidence.

**Do not build an alt-data marketplace.** A third of data buyers say purchased data quality has worsened ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/ai-boom-raises-data-quality-concerns-for-hedge-funds)), and funded data layers own it. Let customers bring their own licences and map them to thesis KPIs.

**The least differentiated parts of today's product** are the terminal and its function codes, the assistant, tool workflows and filing summaries (`what_changed`, `earnings_prep`), standalone bull and bear briefs, LP letter text drafting on its own, the MCP server as plumbing, and the desktop and phone shells. Bloomberg, FactSet, Rogo, Hebbia, Fintool and both labs' finance editions offer close substitutes, and Daloopa, Aiera, FactSet and Moody's all ship MCP servers ([Hebbia blog](https://www.hebbia.com/resources/rogo-competitors)). These are worth maintaining, not expanding. The differentiated core is the set of things that make a record trustworthy: kill criteria checked against XBRL that refuse when data is missing, forecasts that cannot be backdated or withdrawn, a server-written journal, a hash-chained ledger with outside anchors, and DDQ answers built from that record.

## Four shared foundations set the build order

Four pieces of groundwork serve several opportunities, and building them first avoids rework. **Structured decision events** (weight, side and price on every change) feed #1, #2 and #7. Start them immediately: every month without them is history that cannot be reconstructed. **A signed share link with a public chain-verify endpoint** feeds #2 and #8. **E2 wired into Book output** feeds #4 and #6. **Vendor-diligence paperwork** is a sales prerequisite, not a feature: Reg S-P incident-response duties now apply to smaller advisers too (since 3 Jun 2026) and require 72-hour notice from service providers ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/reg-sp-smaller-entities-june-2026-deadline)), and EU managers will need DORA third-party register details.

A reasonable sequence ships the first slices of #1, #3 and #2 in the first month, since all three are mostly in-process maths over existing records. Then #4 and #5, then #6 and #7, with #8 and #9 as opportunistic tests. Two regulatory dates could change the wording of the DDQ and governance reports. The SEC's FY2027 exam priorities are likely around mid-November 2026, given FY2026's 17 Nov 2025 release. A deregulatory rewrite of Rule 204-2's electronic-communications requirements was on the agenda for October 2026 but had not been proposed when the notes were gathered ([reginfo.gov](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&amp=&RIN=3235-AN66)). Pitching "keep the substantive research record, provably unaltered" holds up whichever way that goes. On pricing, anything new on Deal Team that spends outside the AI allowance needs a cap or a price change, because the plan sits at its 25% full-allowance floor (`docs/pricing.md`).

## Conclusion

YouBank's durable advantage is time, not features. Every metric in this plan depends on records that were provably made before the outcome: forecasts that cannot be backdated, a server-written journal, anchored hashes. A competitor can copy the attribution maths in a quarter. It cannot copy a fund's two years of timestamped decisions. That makes the switching cost grow every month a customer stays, and it argues for shipping the record-capturing parts (structured decision events, before-AI forecasts, allocator anchors) before the analytics are polished, because only the clock can produce the history.

The second shift is in who the product serves. Search tools serve the analyst. A decision record serves the analyst, the PM who wants to know whether exits are costing alpha, the CIO who must show AI is worth its budget, the compliance officer facing an exam, and the allocator deciding whether to trust a three-year-old fund. The verification link and the allocator anchor turn that last group into distribution: each manager who raises with YouBank evidence shows the product to the people who vet the next ten managers. With budgets flat and AI spend coming out of other line items, the tools funds keep will be the ones that prove AI improved decisions, and that is the gap this plan targets.
