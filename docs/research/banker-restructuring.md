# Restructuring / Distressed Investment Banking — Market Research for YouBank

*Persona: RX bankers at PJT, Houlihan Lokey (HL), Evercore, Lazard, Moelis, Perella Weinberg (PWP), Guggenheim, Rothschild, Jefferies, Ducera, Ensis and the RX arms of bulge brackets. Covers debtor/company-side, creditor-side (ad hoc groups, official committees) and liability management. Research date: 2026-09-20.*

---

## 1. Role overview and how the group makes money

**What the job is.** RX bankers advise companies that cannot service or refinance their debt (the *debtor* / company side) or the creditors who own that debt (the *creditor* side: ad hoc groups of term-loan lenders or noteholders, official committees of unsecured creditors, sponsors, DIP lenders). The banker's product is a *capital-structure solution* — an amendment, an exchange, a liability management exercise (LME), an out-of-court recapitalization, a prepackaged / prearranged Chapter 11, a free-fall Chapter 11, a §363 sale or a liquidation — plus the analysis and negotiation needed to get creditor consent. Every mandate usually has at least two RX banks: one for the company, one (or several) for creditor constituencies. The debtor mandate is "sell-side-like" (tell the recovery story, maximize enterprise value); creditor mandates are "buy-side-like" (poke holes in projections, build an independent recovery view, push for better treatment, prepare litigation-adjacent arguments if the case turns adversarial). Practitioner tiering as of 2025–26: Tier 1 PJT, HL, Evercore; Tier 2 Lazard, Moelis; Tier 3 Guggenheim, PWP, Rothschild. HL runs the highest-volume shop (1,900+ restructurings, >$4T of claims since 1988, ~150 mandates/yr, creditor-heavy); PJT tends to win flagship debtor cases and led court-approved fee totals in H1'26; Moelis topped Debtwire's 1Q26 IB league table; FTI/A&M dominate the separate "financial advisor / CRO" category.

**Market size (2026).** Debtwire counted 449 US/Canada restructuring advisory mandates covering $130.2bn of debt in 1Q26 (124 mandates / $51.9bn in March alone; out-of-court funded debt +170% MoM while in-court fell 33%). ABI/Epiq: commercial Chapter 11 filings hit 4,589 in H1'26 (+28% y/y; June alone 812, +29%) and Subchapter V elections +50%. Moody's: ~65% of 2025 corporate defaults were distressed exchanges rather than hard defaults; one in four "soft" credit events later ends in hard default, >70% of those within two years — which is the pipeline for 2026–27 "second-bite" mandates. Wachtell (Feb 2026) and Octus's 2026 outlook both expect an uptick in Chapter 11 from failed LMEs, ~$1T of speculative-grade maturities in 2028 and private-credit workouts; stressed sectors named: tech/AI-disrupted software, education, consumer products, healthcare, chemicals, paper/packaging, building products, plus Chinese real estate offshore.

**Mandate types and what drives the fee.**

| Mandate | What the banker does | Timeline | Economics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Out-of-court amend-and-extend / exchange / LME (company side) | Covenant-capacity analysis, structure design (uptier, drop-down, double-dip, exchange), lender outreach, pricing, cooperation-agreement management | 6–16 weeks | Monthly fee + restructuring fee on debt "forgiven or re-tranched"; new-money placement fees |
| Prepackaged Chapter 11 | RSA + plan pre-solicited; DIP/exit financing arranged; first-day support | 30–60 days in court (rapid prepacks 1–14 days; Carestream ~1 month; DISH DBS petition→combined hearing 48 days) | Full restructuring fee; DIP/exit financing fees |
| Prearranged / free-fall Chapter 11 | Plan negotiated post-petition; contested valuation; committee fights | 4–18 months | Monthly fees accumulate; larger restructuring fee; asset-sale fees |
| §363 sale / distressed M&A | Stalking-horse process, bid protections (1–3% break-up fee, typically 2–3%, plus expense reimbursement; 21-day notice), auction | 60–120 days | M&A-style % of consideration |
| Creditor-side (ad hoc group / UCC) | Diligence company plan, independent waterfall, negotiate treatment, DIP/exit terms, KEIP review, testimony | Follows case | Monthly fee + transaction fee, typically paid by the debtor/estate (UCC under §1103 from the estate; ad hoc group fees almost always reimbursed by the company as a condition of the deal) |

**Real fee terms (court filings).**
- PJT / MLN US Holdco (Mitel), S.D. Tex. 2025, debtor side: $200,000 monthly fee (50% credited against the restructuring fee up to a $600,000 cap); capital-raising fees of 1.5% of senior debt, 2.5% of junior/unsecured/"structured" (non-guarantor or unrestricted-sub) financing, 5.0% of equity; DIP fee floor $1.9m; **no fee on rolled-up prepetition debt or take-back exit paper**; restructuring fee $9.8m; total $11.55m for 3.5 months (petition Mar 9, confirmed Apr 17, effective Jun 20, 2025).
- HL / Delta Air Lines UCC: $200,000 monthly + $5.0m transaction fee on confirmation of a committee-approved plan, monthly fees credited 100% after month 24.
- Evercore / Chisholm Oil & Gas: ~$4m total ≈ 0.8% of prepetition debt; Evercore / McDermott: >$87m on ~$5bn of debt (in-court capped at $27.5m). HL / Carestream prepack: $6.89m.
- Rules of thumb: top debtor-side retainers $150–300k/month; completion fees low single-digit % of debt (sample engagement letter: 0.625% of debt forgiven/re-tranched up to $50m, 0.875% above); consent/lock-up fees to creditors 0.25–3% (majority <1%); ad hoc group work fees 0.05–1.75%; backstop fees 0.25–5% in European schemes and ~6–14% on US plan rights offerings.

The economics are counter-cyclical, lumpy and back-end-loaded: retainers cover cost, the restructuring fee is the profit, and Chapter 11 fees are only earned if approved under §328(a)/§330 in a final order.

## 2. Day-to-day tasks by seniority

Deal teams are lean (MD + VP + Associate + Analyst); analysts routinely run the model solo on a $500m–$5bn capital structure and work 70–90 hour weeks. Every situation has a unique cap structure, so there are fewer templates than in M&A and "endless re-jiggering" of decks as negotiations move.

**Analyst (comp ~$150–220k all-in).**
- *Capital structure table* ("cap table"): every tranche with entity, amount, rate, maturity, security, guarantors, trading price, YTW, leverage-through. Built at pitch, refreshed weekly (daily during a live process) from Bloomberg/CapIQ/Octus plus the credit agreement.
- *Screens and profiles*: one-page screens (cap table + footnotes) and 1–3 page profiles (trading charts, liquidity table, catalysts) on 20–50 names the MD is tracking; refreshed monthly, spiked on earnings/8-K events. PJT's RSSG even has a dedicated "New Business Research" analyst role.
- *Liquidity analysis*: cash + revolver availability − letters of credit − restricted/trapped cash; monthly/quarterly runway to the "cash cliff". Weekly in a live case.
- *13-week cash flow (13WCF) work*: the company's FA/CRO (A&M, FTI, AlixPartners) usually owns the 13WCF; the banker stress-tests it, layers DIP draws, professional fees and the case calendar, and reconciles the weekly variance report against DIP-order tests. Weekly.
- *Recovery waterfall / value-break model* for each scenario (base, downside, plan, liquidation, LME-participant vs holdout). Iterated daily during term-sheet negotiations.
- *Leverage, coverage and covenant compliance* schedules, covenant headroom by quarter, maturity wall.
- *Trading comps / precedent transactions*, precedent LME and restructuring comps (Octus/Debtwire), DIP and exit financing comps (pricing, fees, roll-up ratios).
- *Court/process support*: capital-structure and "events leading to Chapter 11" sections of the first-day declaration; disclosure-statement exhibits (valuation analysis, liquidation analysis, financial projections); docket monitoring; data-room and diligence-list management; KEIP/KERP metric benchmarking; fee-application time detail.
- Lender/board presentation pages, notes from creditor calls, comparison of competing proposals.

**Associate (comp ~$250–350k; HL posts $175k base).** Owns the model and deck; drafts term sheets and restructuring proposals; runs LME structuring math (basket capacity, uptier participation thresholds, pro forma priority ladders); coordinates covenant readings with counsel; runs creditor Q&A and diligence; prepares management for creditor meetings; builds DIP sizing and exit-financing books; drafts declarations supporting DIP, bidding procedures and plan; associates in RX stay in the numbers far longer than M&A associates.

**VP (~$400–700k).** Runs the process day to day with counsel, the FA/CRO and the client: sets the case calendar and RSA milestones, negotiates term-sheet economics, markets the DIP and exit financing, manages the ad hoc group dialogue, marks up every deliverable, sits on all management/creditor calls, prepares MDs for testimony and writes the storyline of the board decks.

**Director / MD (~$1–3m+).** Origination: monitors names for years ahead of distress, pitches boards and special committees, gets hired by ad hoc groups (often via the law firm). Negotiates with principals, decides structure and strategy, presents to the board on fiduciary alternatives, testifies (valuation, DIP marketing, sale process, plan feasibility), negotiates fees and the engagement letter, manages conflicts (one side per deal).

## 3. Data and documents used

**Public filings (EDGAR).** 8-K Item 1.03 (bankruptcy/receivership), Item 2.04 (triggering event accelerating a direct financial obligation), Item 1.01/2.03 (RSA, forbearance, new facility), Item 7.01/8.01 "cleansing" 8-Ks releasing the term sheets and projections shared with restricted creditors at the end of an NDA period (practitioner convention); 10-K/10-Q debt footnotes, liquidity and going-concern language, covenant disclosures; credit agreements, indentures, intercreditor agreements and amendments filed as exhibits.

**Court records.** PACER/CM-ECF ($0.10/page, capped per doc); free claims-agent microsites (Kroll, Epiq, Stretto, Verita, Omni) for large cases; CourtListener/RECAP; court opinions. Documents ranked by practitioners: first-day declaration + disclosure statement (~80% of the information), DIP motion/interim and final orders and cash collateral orders (budget, milestones, roll-up, carve-out, challenge period, 506(c)/552(b) waivers), RSA (milestones, support %), plan (classes/treatment), schedules and SOFAs (intercompany claims), KEIP/KERP motions, bidding procedures and stalking-horse APA, fee applications, monthly operating reports, DIP variance certifications.

**Subscription data.** Octus (formerly Reorg: dockets, covenant analysis, capital structures, LME/DIP databases, "CreditAI" over public + private data-room documents since Mar 2026); Debtwire/ION (mandates, DIP financing reports, co-op tracking); 9fin; LevFin Insights; CreditSights; Bloomberg (DDIS maturity distribution, CAST seniority view, 65,000+ issuers, history to 2011; bond prices via TRACE, loan marks via Markit/IHS, CDS); Capital IQ capital structure details from filings; PitchBook LCD (loan indexes, secondary prices, default rates); Moody's/S&P recovery and default studies; Epiq AACER (PACER-derived filing data feeds).

**Fields that matter per tranche.** Borrower/issuer entity and its position in the org chart; guarantors (loan parties vs non-loan parties; restricted vs unrestricted subsidiaries); collateral and lien priority (1L/1.5L/2L, ABL vs term collateral, intercreditor standstill/waterfall); amount outstanding, currency; coupon (base rate + spread, floor, cash/PIK toggle), OID, call protection/make-whole, ECF sweep; maturity including *springing maturities* (e.g., RCF or TLB springs 91 days inside a junior maturity); financial covenants (maintenance vs springing at 35–40% revolver usage, level, step-downs, equity cure) and the *defined* EBITDA (add-back caps historically 15–25%, synergy windows 18–24 months; S&P found realized EBITDA ~30% below inception projections); baskets (restricted payments, investments/unrestricted-sub, debt, liens, general, builder); amendment thresholds (Required Lenders >50%; sacred rights; indenture 66⅔% for collateral release, unanimity on payment terms); "open market purchase" and pro rata sharing language; blockers (J.Crew, Serta, Chewy, Pluralsight/double-dip); change-of-control put; MFN sunsets; ratings; market price, YTW, trading volume; holder lists (co-op membership, sponsor cross-holdings).

## 4. Core analyses and methodology

**4.1 Capital structure table.** Rows = each tranche in priority order, grouped by obligor group; columns = face, market price, market value, rate, maturity, security, guarantors, cumulative leverage (gross and net), leverage through the tranche at the *defined* EBITDA and at "clean" EBITDA, cash interest, YTW and implied EV at current prices. Footnote every mismatch between filings and the credit agreement (accrued PIK, undrawn commitments, LCs, hedges, factoring, leases, litigation, pension and tax claims that will become claims in a case).

**4.2 Leverage and coverage.** Total and net leverage at each priority layer; cash interest coverage (EBITDA − capex)/cash interest; FCCR; PIK share of coupon; "burn" = EBITDA − cash interest − capex − taxes − working capital − mandatory amortization; sensitivity to SOFR and to add-back haircuts.

**4.3 Liquidity runway and 13WCF.** Direct-method weekly receipts (by customer/collection curve) and disbursements (payroll, vendors, rent, interest, taxes, capex, professional fees), opening/ending cash, revolver draws and availability (borrowing base for ABLs), minimum liquidity covenant, and the *trough week*. In Chapter 11 the 13WCF becomes the DIP budget: rolling forecast refreshed every 4 weeks, weekly variance reporting, permitted variances typically 15–20% on receipts and tighter on disbursements (Alkegen: cumulative disbursements ≤112.5% of budget from week 4; minimum liquidity $32.5m). Output: months of runway and the date by which a deal or filing is required.

**4.4 Debt maturity wall.** Amount by year and tranche, with springing maturities shown at the earlier date; overlay of refinancing conditions (market yields, ratings) to determine which maturities cannot be refinanced without a discount.

**4.5 Covenant headroom.** Recompute each maintenance/springing covenant on the document's definitions; headroom = (covenant level − actual)/level and the EBITDA decline that breaches it (direct-lending deals are typically set with 25–35% cushion; BSL springing tests trigger above ~35–40% revolver usage). Add capacity analysis: maximum pari and priming debt today (ratio + free-and-clear + fully drawn revolver), RP and investment capacity, unrestricted-sub capacity — this is the same work an LME planner and an LME defender both do.

**4.6 Recovery waterfall.** Inputs: distributable value (plan EV + excess cash − admin/DIP), claims by class and by legal entity. Order under the absolute priority rule: DIP super-priority → administrative and professional fees (§503(b)) → secured claims to the value of collateral (§506; the deficiency drops to unsecured; §1111(b) election) → priority unsecured (§507: taxes, wages) → general unsecured (funded unsecured, trade, rejection damages, litigation) → subordinated → preferred → common. Build it entity-by-entity to capture structural subordination and guarantee coverage, then consolidate. The *fulcrum* is the most senior class not paid in full; it typically receives the reorganized equity. Worked example (WSP): $200m EBITDA × 3.5x = $700m TEV against $200m bank debt, $300m senior notes, $300m subordinated → bank 100%, senior 100%, sub 66.7% = fulcrum. Plan value distribution: fulcrum class takes new equity, senior classes reinstated or take-back paper, "tips" (warrants/small equity) to junior classes to buy peace, and a rights offering at a discount to plan value with a backstop fee (academic average 6% of the raise with ~50% 3-month returns to backstop parties; recent deals 8–14%: Hearthside 10%, Hornblower 10%, Azul 14%, United Site Services 8%; ConvergeOne's 10% backstop plus discounted direct investment gave majority lenders 31% more than excluded lenders and was reversed on equal-treatment grounds in Sept 2025 — any exclusive backstop now needs a market test).

**4.7 Valuation in bankruptcy.** Judges accept DCF, trading comps and precedent transactions as the "standard" methods; the fight is over comp selection, projections and discount rates, so bankers present ranges and a midpoint that support (or attack) the plan. Liquidation analysis for the best-interests test applies recovery factors to each asset class (cash 100%, receivables 60–80%, inventory/PP&E lower, intangibles near zero) less wind-down and Chapter 7 trustee costs. Expert reports and testimony are a core MD deliverable in contested confirmations.

**4.8 Distressed exchange / LME math.**
- *Uptier*: Required Lenders (>50%) amend to permit super-priority debt; participants exchange (often at a discount) into new first-out tranches; non-participants become last-out. Model participant vs holdout recovery under the pro forma priority ladder, the consent/exchange premium and litigation risk. Serta (5th Cir., Dec 31 2024; cert denied Nov 10 2025): a privately negotiated non-pro-rata exchange was not an "open market purchase"; Mitel (NY App. Div.) upheld an uptier on different "purchase by way of assignment" language; Incora was held to strip liens without the required two-thirds consent. Uptier blockers rose from ~40% to ~85% of loans post-Serta; sacred-rights protection against lien subordination from ~10% to ~70%; drop-down blockers remain rare (~9%).
- *Drop-down*: use investment/RP capacity to move IP or a division to an unrestricted subsidiary and raise structurally senior debt there (J.Crew, 2016–17; Synthomer and Optimum in 2026). Math: capacity stack (ratio + builder + general baskets) vs asset value moved; recovery of remaining collateral for legacy lenders.
- *Double-dip / pari-plus*: new loan to a non-guarantor sub, on-lent to the credit group; the lender gets a guarantee claim (dip 1) and a pledged intercompany receivable (dip 2), or in pari-plus an additional guarantee from outside the group (At Home, Trinseo, Sabre). Recovery = par claim counted twice against the same collateral pool.
- *Exit consents / coercive exchanges*: exchange offer conditioned on tendering holders consenting to strip covenants (majority) while payment terms need unanimity and collateral release 66⅔%; "ticking" or speed-based pricing (Cable One/MBI: first 50% into first-out at par, later participants into second-out — abandoned after a >65% cooperation agreement formed). Antolín (2026): reinstated at par to 2035 at 6.97% vs 67.5% of face to 2030 at 8.28% stepping up 200 bps/yr.
- *Cooperation agreements*: 51% for optionality up to ~80–100% (DISH DBS ~80%; Evans Delivery 100%); 12–18 month terms with extensions; the banker for the co-op sizes the blocking position and models the "join-or-die" payoff matrix.

**4.9 DIP financing sizing and pricing.** Size = 13-week trough + minimum liquidity cushion ($10–75m) + professional-fee carve-out + adequate-protection payments + case costs (KEIP, critical vendor, utilities deposits) + contingency, split into new money and roll-up. Roll-up ratios run 1:1 to 3:1 (Alkegen $315m new / $315m roll-up; STG 48.9% roll-up ratio; Axip 75.7% "creeping" roll-up); creeping roll-ups appeared in 4 of 16 facilities in Jan–Feb 2026, and 13 of 16 DIPs came from prepetition lenders. Pricing: defensive lender DIPs SOFR+400–600; priming/third-party SOFR+700–1,200; all-in 12–18% including fees; commitment fees 2–5%, exit fees 1–3%, unused 50–100 bps; backstop/structuring premiums 5–10% in large PIK-heavy deals (Saks: $2.56bn, SOFR+11% PIK new money, 8% structuring, 10% backstop; Alkegen: SOFR+8.375% half PIK, 5% backstop, 2.5% upfront, 2.25% exit; Pretium: SOFR+5.25% with equity-based fees). Timing: interim order 24–72 hours with 20–40% availability, final 21–45 days, maturity 6–18 months (90–180 days for 363 cases), DIP-to-exit conversion common (Alkegen → $400m exit). Bankers also produce the "DIP marketing" declaration showing outreach to third-party lenders.

**4.10 Pro forma capital structure, feasibility and best interests.** Pro forma cap table at emergence (reinstated debt, take-back paper, new-money exit facility, equity from equitization and rights offering) with leverage, coverage and liquidity at exit; feasibility (§1129(a)(11)) shown via 5-year projections, covenant compliance and liquidity under downside cases; best-interests test (§1129(a)(7)) comparing each class's plan recovery to hypothetical Chapter 7 liquidation; cramdown (§1129(b)) fair-and-equitable / unfair-discrimination analysis when a class rejects; KEIP metrics benchmarked to the plan (EBITDA, liquidity, emergence date, sale price) so they pass the Dana factors under §503(c)(3).

## 6. Key terms glossary

1. **Absolute priority rule** — senior classes are paid in full before junior classes receive anything (§1129(b)).
2. **Ad hoc group** — informal group of like creditors (e.g., TLB lenders) that hires counsel and a banker to negotiate; fees usually reimbursed by the company.
3. **Adequate protection** — replacement liens, cash payments or superpriority claims given to prepetition secured lenders for use of collateral.
4. **Amend-and-extend (A&E)** — maturity extension in exchange for fee/spread.
5. **Backstop** — commitment to buy unsubscribed rights-offering securities for a fee (typically 6–14% in recent cases).
6. **Best-interests test** — §1129(a)(7): each dissenting holder receives at least its Chapter 7 liquidation value.
7. **Carve-out** — DIP collateral reserved for professional fees and US Trustee fees.
8. **Cash collateral** — lenders' collateral proceeds used post-petition with consent or court order.
9. **Challenge period** — window (often 60–75 days) for a committee to attack prepetition liens.
10. **Cooperation agreement (co-op)** — lenders agree to act together and refuse non-pro-rata deals.
11. **Cov-lite** — no maintenance covenants; only springing revolver test.
12. **Cramdown** — confirming a plan over a rejecting class if "fair and equitable" and not unfairly discriminatory.
13. **Creeping roll-up** — each DIP draw automatically converts an equal amount of prepetition debt into DIP debt.
14. **DIP financing** — post-petition superpriority loan under §364.
15. **Disclosure statement** — court-approved plan information including projections, valuation and liquidation analysis.
16. **Distressed exchange** — exchange of debt at a discount or for junior paper; counted as a default by rating agencies.
17. **Double-dip / pari-plus** — new-money structure creating two claims (guarantee + pledged intercompany loan) against the same collateral.
18. **Drop-down** — moving assets to an unrestricted subsidiary to raise structurally senior debt.
19. **Equitization** — converting debt claims into reorganized equity.
20. **Exit consent** — tendering holders vote to strip covenants from the old notes.
21. **Feasibility** — §1129(a)(11): plan not likely to be followed by liquidation or further reorganization.
22. **First-day motions / declaration** — initial relief (cash management, wages, critical vendors, DIP) and the sworn narrative of the case.
23. **Fulcrum security** — most senior class impaired; receives the equity.
24. **Intercreditor agreement** — priority, standstill and waterfall terms between lien classes.
25. **KEIP / KERP** — incentive vs retention plans; insiders' retention is limited by §503(c)(1); KEIPs are judged under §503(c)(3) and the Dana factors.
26. **Liability management exercise (LME)** — out-of-court transaction using document flexibility (uptier, drop-down, double-dip, exchange).
27. **Make-whole** — call premium; litigated when debt is accelerated in bankruptcy.
28. **Milestones** — RSA/DIP deadlines for filing, DS approval, confirmation, effective date, sale.
29. **Monthly operating report (MOR)** — post-petition financials filed with the court.
30. **Plan of reorganization (POR)** — classification and treatment of claims; voting requires 2/3 in amount and 1/2 in number per accepting class (§1126(c)).
31. **Prepack / prearranged / free-fall** — plan solicited before filing / agreed but solicited after / no deal at filing.
32. **Priming lien** — DIP lien senior to existing liens (§364(d)).
33. **Restricted (creditor)** — holder under NDA receiving MNPI; cannot trade until cleansed.
34. **Restructuring support agreement (RSA)** — lock-up with treatment term sheet, milestones and termination events.
35. **Roll-up** — prepetition debt converted into DIP debt (1:1 to 3:1 new money).
36. **Sacred rights** — amendments requiring each affected lender's consent (payment terms, pro rata, waterfall, collateral release).
37. **§363 sale** — court-approved asset sale free and clear, often with a stalking horse and 2–3% break-up fee.
38. **Springing maturity** — earlier maturity triggered if a junior tranche remains outstanding inside a set window.
39. **Take-back paper** — new debt issued to creditors under the plan in partial satisfaction of claims.
40. **Uptier** — majority lenders exchange into new super-priority debt, subordinating holdouts.
41. **Unrestricted subsidiary** — entity outside the covenant group and guarantee/collateral package.
42. **Value break** — the point in the capital structure where EV is exhausted.

## 8. Prompt library (what an RX banker would actually ask)

1. "Build the capital structure table for [Company] from the latest 10-K and 8-Ks, ordered by priority, with leverage through each tranche at reported and covenant EBITDA. Footnote anything you could not tie out."
2. "Which entities guarantee the 2029 secured notes, and which subsidiaries are non-guarantors or unrestricted? Show the org chart."
3. "Under the credit agreement filed as Exhibit 10.1 on [date], what is the maximum incremental pari passu secured debt the borrower can incur today, assuming the revolver is fully drawn? Cite sections."
4. "Does this credit agreement contain a J.Crew blocker, a Serta/pro rata protection, a Chewy blocker or a double-dip blocker? Quote the language."
5. "What are the Required Lender threshold and the sacred rights? Could a majority subordinate the liens of non-consenting lenders?"
6. "Compute covenant headroom for the springing net leverage test for the last four quarters and the EBITDA decline that would breach it."
7. "Show the maturity wall for [Company] including any springing maturities and the trigger conditions."
8. "Run a recovery waterfall at EV of $X, $Y and $Z. Identify the fulcrum and the implied equity split if the fulcrum class takes 100% of new equity less a 5% MIP and a 15% rights offering at a 30% discount to plan value."
9. "Estimate general unsecured claims from the schedules and SOFAs in [case], separating trade, lease rejection, litigation and intercompany."
10. "Summarize the first-day declaration in [case]: capital structure, events leading to filing, DIP terms, RSA support and milestones."
11. "Extract the DIP terms from the interim order: new money, roll-up, rate, fees, maturity, milestones, minimum liquidity, variance tests, carve-out, challenge period, 506(c)/552(b) waivers."
12. "Compare this DIP's pricing and fees to DIPs of $200–600m filed in the last 12 months."
13. "Draft a term sheet for an uptier exchange in which participating lenders exchange at 90 into a first-out tranche; show participant vs holdout recoveries at EV of $X and the litigation risks given the open-market-purchase language."
14. "What investment and restricted-payment capacity could fund a drop-down of the [brand/IP] to an unrestricted subsidiary, and what would legacy lenders' collateral coverage be afterwards?"
15. "List the cooperation agreements and ad hoc groups reported for [Company] and their advisors and reported holdings."
16. "Build a liquidation analysis for the best-interests test using recovery factors from three recent [sector] disclosure statements; cite them."
17. "Prepare the pro forma capital structure at emergence under the plan term sheet and test feasibility on the projections with a 20% EBITDA downside."
18. "Benchmark the proposed KEIP (metrics, cost as % of salary, participants) against 10 recent cases and the Dana factors."
19. "Pull the retention applications in [case] and summarize each banker's monthly fee, restructuring fee, financing fees and credits."
20. "Generate a one-page distress profile for [Company]: cap table, liquidity, covenant headroom, maturities, trading levels and the last three catalysts."
