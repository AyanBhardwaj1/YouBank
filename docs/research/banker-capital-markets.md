## 1. Role overview: how each desk makes money

Capital markets groups sit between investment banking coverage (which owns the client relationship) and sales & trading (which owns the investor relationship). They are product groups: they execute one category of transaction over and over, which is why Mergers & Inquisitions describes the work as "PowerPoint drafting market update slides and sharing information with different groups" and "far less modeling-intensive" than M&A, with hours closer to "7 AM to 7 PM" and MD pay "20-25% lower" because fees are "split between multiple banks." LevFin is the exception: hours and modeling are M&A-like because the clients are private equity firms.

### 1.1 Equity Capital Markets (ECM)

**Sub-teams.** (1) Equity origination: pitches and executes IPOs and follow-ons; (2) Syndicate: runs the order book, allocates, stabilizes, coordinates the other banks; (3) Convertibles / equity-linked; (4) at some banks, private placements. Products span IPOs, marketed / confidentially marketed / accelerated follow-ons, secondaries, blocks and bought deals, ATMs, rights offerings, converts, and accelerated share repurchases.

**Origination vs. syndicate.** Origination bankers win the mandate and run the issuer; the syndicate desk prices and allocates on behalf of the whole underwriting group and manages the greenshoe and stabilization. Bookrunners (typically bulge brackets) earn most of the economics; co-managers earn a slice of the selling concession.

**Fee levels (gross spread).**
- **IPOs.** Jay Ritter's data (2001-2025, 2,522 bookbuilt IPOs): for deals raising $30-160M (2025 dollars), **93.3% carried exactly 7.0%**; for deals above $160M, 54.4% were below 7% and 45.1% were at 7%; for $160-200M deals 86.1% were still exactly 7%. Mega-deals price far lower: Visa 2.8% ($17.9B), General Motors 0.75% ($15.8B), Facebook 1.1% ($16B), Uber 1.3% ($8.1B), Alibaba 1.2%. Live 2026 examples from EDGAR: **Cerebras Systems** (May 2026) sold 30.0M shares at $185 ($5.55B) with a $4.3475/share discount, a **2.35% gross spread ($130.4M)**, a 4.5M-share (15%) greenshoe and a 1.5M-share directed share program, bookrun by Morgan Stanley, Citi, Barclays and UBS with ten co-managers; **Gloo Holdings** (July 2026) sold 7.0M shares at $3.25 ($22.75M) at a **6.0% spread** (Citizens, Roth).
- **Spread split.** The convention is 20% management fee / 20% underwriting fee / 60% selling concession; Torstila's study of 4,186 1990s IPOs found averages of 20.8% / 21.8% / 57.4%, with the lead manager capturing about two-thirds of the total spread in the worked example because it books most of the selling concession.
- **All-in IPO cost.** IPO Hub's build-up for a $200M IPO: $14M spread plus $2.5-4M company counsel, $1.5-2.5M underwriters' counsel, $2-3.5M audit/comfort, $2-5M first-year D&O, printer/SEC/exchange/transfer-agent fees, for a total of **$22-30M (11-15% of proceeds)**; ongoing public-company cost $8-15M per year.
- **Follow-ons and blocks.** Marketed follow-ons typically price at a 3-8% discount to last close over a 5-10 day book; overnight bought deals at 6-12%; blocks bid over a few hours at 6-10%. Live example: **Aveanna Healthcare** secondary (Aug 2026, sellers Bain Capital and J.H. Whitney), 15.0M shares at $11.75 versus a $13.16 last sale (**10.7% discount**), $0.25/share discount (**2.13% spread**), RBC sole bookrunner, 30-day lock-up, sponsor ownership falling to 44.1% and ending controlled-company status.
- **Converts.** ICR: 5-7 year maturities, "low single-digit coupons," 25-40% conversion premiums; 57% of recent deals add a call spread (bond hedge + warrant, or capped call) that lifts the effective premium to "75-100%+"; the U.S. market is about $270-280B across 560-570 securities; 2024 issuance ran $70-80B versus a normal $50-60B. Practitioner convention is a 2-2.75% gross spread on converts (not verified from a 2026 filing in this research).
- **SPACs.** Live 2026 example (Southern Cross Acquisition II, Aug 2026): $75M at $10.00/unit (share + warrant + 1/4 right), **$10.025/unit in trust**, $0.11/unit upfront discount plus 150,000 representative shares, 2.875M founder shares (~25% post-IPO) bought for $25,000, $2.06M sponsor private placement, 12 months to complete a combination, $11.50 warrant strike, Nasdaq Capital Market. Classic economics were a 20% promote and a 2% upfront / 3.5% deferred underwriting fee; post-2022 structures shifted to 10-15% performance-linked promotes, smaller warrant coverage and redemption rates of 80-98%, with committed PIPEs doing the real underwriting. Nasdaq raised SPAC listing thresholds effective May 15, 2026 (Global Market $75M to $100M market value of listed securities; Capital Market SPACs $75M MVLS, $20M unrestricted public float, 400 round-lot holders, 4 market makers). 2025 saw roughly 138-150 SPAC IPOs raising $25.8B (about 40% of U.S. IPO count); Renaissance's stats page shows 150 SPAC IPOs year-to-date 2026.

**League tables.** Dealogic and LSEG publish ECM/DCM/loan rankings by proceeds with full credit to each bookrunner; GlobalCapital republishes them by product. Dealogic put JPMorgan first in 2025 global IB fees at $10.1B (8.4% share). H1-2026 rankings were not retrievable without a subscription.

**2026 market backdrop.** Renaissance Capital: 1Q26 had 35 IPOs raising $9.9B; 2Q26 had **48 IPOs raising a record $104.8B**, including SpaceX's $75B IPO (up 19% day one, $1.7T market cap) and nine other $1B+ deals led by Cerebras; $100M+ IPOs averaged +20% from offer; the Renaissance IPO Index gained 42% in the quarter versus 15% for the S&P 500; Anthropic and OpenAI filed confidentially. Year-to-date (through mid-September) Renaissance counts 109 IPOs and $146.5B of proceeds. Ritter's 2025 data: 90 operating-company IPOs, mean first-day return 29.3%, $13.1B "money left on the table" on $39.0B of proceeds. SIFMA's 2026 Fact Book: 2025 U.S. equity issuance $232.6B, IPOs $47.0B (+50%), follow-ons $154.6B.

### 1.2 Debt Capital Markets (DCM)

**What it is.** Investment-grade (and sovereign/agency/FIG) bond origination and syndication. M&I: "a higher-volume, lower-margin business"; teams split corporate vs. government and by industry vertical, and by function (origination advises the client; syndicate "allocate[s] investor orders and build[s] bond offering books").

**How a deal prices.** Announce with initial price thoughts (IPT) expressed as a spread to the benchmark Treasury; collect indications; tighten to guidance; launch at a final spread; price; settle T+2 to T+10. The new-issue concession (NIC) versus the issuer's secondary curve is the key negotiated number: investmentgrade.com reports NICs of roughly 3-7 bps in Q1 2026 versus 8-12 bps post-pandemic; IG OAS was 81 bps and effective yield 5.53% on 1 Sept 2026 (BBB 99 bps, HY 265 bps).

**Fee levels (verified from 2026 prospectus supplements).** Fees are quoted as a percent of principal and scale with tenor, not size:
- **AbbVie (A2/A-, Aug 2026, $10.0B, nine tranches):** 0.150% on 2-year fixed and floating, 0.250% (2030), 0.350% (2031), 0.375% (2033), 0.450% (2036), 0.475% (2038), 0.750% (30- and 40-year). Total discount $41.8M = 0.42% of proceeds. Pricing: T+32 (2028) to T+92 (2066); make-whole T+5 to T+15; par calls 1-6 months before maturity; T+10 settlement.
- **Xylem (Baa2/BBB, Sept 2026, $1.5B acquisition financing):** 0.450% (3-year, T+50), 0.600% (5-year, T+65), 0.650% (10-year, T+85); make-whole T+10/T+10/T+15.
- **High yield:** typically 1.25-2.5% of principal (practitioner range; not verified from a 2026 filing here). Third-party sources quoting "1-2% for IG" overstate today's IG fees by a factor of 2-4x.

**Volumes.** SIFMA: 2025 U.S. corporate bond issuance $2.2T (+12.5%); Q1 2026 was $775.2B, the largest quarter since Q2 2020, of which roughly $620B was IG.

**Adjacent DCM products:** liability management (tenders, exchanges, consents), ratings advisory, hybrid capital, private placements (4(a)(2)/144A), commercial paper programs, and derivatives overlays (pre-issuance hedges).

### 1.3 Leveraged Finance (LevFin)

**What it is.** Sub-investment-grade debt: leveraged loans (RC, TLA, TLB, second lien), high-yield bonds, bridge commitments, and increasingly the bank's own private-credit/direct-lending arm. Clients are mostly financial sponsors funding LBOs, add-ons, refinancings and dividend recaps.

**How it makes money.** Underwriting/arrangement fees on committed financings, upfront fees on best-efforts deals, bridge commitment fees, plus secondary trading revenue. PitchBook LCD: "a new leveraged loan can carry an arranger fee of 1-5% of the total loan commitment"; practitioner ranges quoted on WSO put first-lien TLB underwriting fees at 2.00-2.25% and second-lien at 2.50-3.00%. Market flex protects the underwriter: pricing flex commonly runs 100-150 bps with a cap on how much may be taken as OID (one cited structure: 125-150 bps total, no more than 62.5 bps as OID, converted to yield over a 4-year average life). Davis Polk describes 25 bp step-ups at 120 and/or 180 days after signing if syndication is not complete, a 25 bp ratings flex if ratings come in below assumption, and ticking fees of 0% for 30 days, 50% of margin for the next 30, then 100% of margin plus floor. Cleary: European caps are "usually in the range of 1 to 1.5%," pricing flex must be matched by covenant headroom adjustments, documentary flex commonly covers incremental baskets, MFN, call protection, ratchets and synergy add-back caps, and reverse flex returns upside to the borrower when books are oversubscribed.

**2026 backdrop (Fidelity/LCD, Q2 2026).** Institutional loan volume ~$224B (Q1: ~$240B), **~70% refinancing/repricing**; sponsored issuance down 33% to the lowest since 4Q23 while higher-rated corporates raised $53.6B (5-year high), including Warner Bros. Discovery's $13B loan, the largest institutional loan since 2008; B- spreads widened 55 bps in the quarter; healthcare took a record 12.4% share while software fell to 9%. Loan yields 8.21% and OAS 453 bps (June 2026); HY OAS 275 (BB 162, B 293, CCC 960); SOFR 3.73%. Loan spread-to-worst conventionally "includes accretion of OID and assumes a 4-year life."

**Private credit competition.** Direct lending is now $1.5-2T, roughly the size of the BSL market (Cleary); global private credit AUM $1.7T with direct lending ~$850B (CT Acquisitions). In 2025, $36.9B of syndicated loans were refinanced into direct lending (+18%); the average unitranche coupon was ~9.0% at end-2025, ~160 bps above comparable BSL; new-issue unitranche spreads compressed to ~S+525 in Q1 2026 from 650-700 in 2023, with all-in coupons of 9.0-11.0% versus 7.5-9.5% for BSL. Structural trade-offs: unitranche keeps financial maintenance covenants and 12 months @101 call protection, versus cov-lite and 6 months @101 for TLBs; private credit wins on speed and certainty (no flex), banks win on price and scale. Cliffwater's CDLI (asset-weighted, quarterly, built from BDC 10-K/10-Q filings; $549B, ~21,000 loans) returned 9.3% in 2025 with a 9.5% twenty-year average and one negative year (2008); PIK was 7.3% of income.

### 1.4 Financial Sponsors Group (FSG)

**What it is.** A coverage group whose "industry" is private equity (plus hedge funds, sovereign funds, family offices). It is the relationship hub between the sponsor, the industry group, LevFin, ECM and M&A. Because "your clients need to do deals to stay in business," less selling is needed, but PE clients "will not hesitate to ask you for model/presentation updates or make other requests at 3 AM" (M&I).

**How it makes money.** Wallet share across everything a sponsor does: acquisition financing (every LBO needs debt), sell-side M&A and IPO exits, dividend recaps, secondaries/blocks, fund finance, and portfolio-company services. Kroll, RBC, PNC and others market dedicated sponsor coverage for deal flow and financing.

**Teams.** Relationship-focused FSGs summarize sponsor holdings, track which portfolio companies are ripe for sale, and benchmark multiples, leverage and terms; execution-focused FSGs (bulge brackets) build initial LBO models, data rooms and CIMs. Sponsor-specific metrics tracked: TVPI, DPI, RVPI, dry powder, fund vintage and remaining investment period, fee-related earnings and EV/AUM for listed managers.

**Dividend recaps as the 2026 sponsor product.** LCD/PitchBook: sponsored dividend-recap loan volume was $74.3B in 2025 (+11% after +326% in 2024); Q1 2026 fell 54.5% to $11B but July 2026 alone saw $9.11B, the most since September 2025; average recap leverage 5.04x in 2026 (5.2x in 2025), with 22% of deals at 6x or more. Solvency opinions test balance sheet, cash flow and capital adequacy.

### 1.5 Private Capital Advisory (PCA) and private placements

**What it is.** Fund placement (primary raises for GPs), GP-led and LP-led secondaries, continuation vehicles, NAV/fund finance, GP-stakes advisory, and direct company placements (growth equity, pre-IPO/crossover rounds, PIPEs, structured equity). Piper Sandler's PCA cites 300+ fund raises totaling $500B+, 70+ GP-led deals and coverage of 5,000+ LPs; its equity private placements team lists 50+ deals in five years, mostly $100-700M. Advisory Ranking's 2026 top-20 placement agents are led by Atlantic-Pacific, Asante, Monument Group, Probitas and Rede Partners. Fees are a percentage of capital raised (retainers plus success fees); primary placement work resembles ECM in cadence, secondaries resemble M&A (M&I).

Citi's September 2026 ECM associate posting ($175-225K base, Series 7/79/63 within 150 days) emphasizes evaluating "corporate transactions and financial structures" and committee process; ECM analysts at bulge brackets "execute public capital raising transactions... including IPOs and follow-on offerings" and prepare "pitches and RFPs."

**IPO process checkpoints where analysis is produced:** organizational meeting (kick-off comps and structure); confidential S-1 submission (EGCs; SEC first comments typically ~30 days, with 60-90 days to clear per Orrick); public flip and testing-the-waters; launch with a price range; 8-10 day roadshow; pricing (final price vs. range, upsizing, greenshoe); allocation; 30-day stabilization window; 180-day lock-up (with increasingly common early-release triggers); first earnings.

**Convertibles:** model coupon vs. premium trade-off, implied volatility versus realized, delta (M&I's worked example uses ~53%), bond floor and parity; structure capped calls (strike at the cap) and warrants; settle on net-share vs. flexible settlement for EPS treatment.

**Dual-track analysis:** IPO value (public comps less an IPO discount, partial sell-down, lock-up, aftermarket risk) versus M&A value (control premium, synergies, certainty, full exit). Orrick: the auction customarily launches with the initial S-1 filing; the decision to sell is normally made before the roadshow; separate data rooms and confidentiality (other underwriters may not know an M&A process exists) are standard. Cooley (March 2026) and DealLawyers (April 2026) describe dual-tracks as back in favor as sponsors' exit backlogs persist.

## 4. Core methodologies

**IPO discount and aftermarket analysis.** Bankers value the issuer on trading comps, then apply an IPO discount (conventionally 10-15%) to leave a first-day return; Ritter's data show why: across 1980-2025, 28% of IPOs priced below range, 49% within and 23% above, with average first-day returns of about 3% / 12% / 50% respectively (the "partial adjustment" effect). 2025's mean first-day return was 29.3% and 2024's 15.3%. Aftermarket analysis tracks return from offer at day 1/30/90, greenshoe exercise, and performance against the IPO index (Renaissance: 2Q26 $100M+ IPOs +20% from offer). Unity Biotechnology's SEC correspondence lists the price-range methodology bankers actually use: comparable public companies, "comparable company valuations at the time of their respective IPOs," "valuation increases for comparable companies at the time of their respective IPOs," testing-the-waters feedback, recent financings, business developments and underwriters' market read.

**IPO valuation.** Trading comps (EV/revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E on NTM), precedent IPOs (multiple at pricing and step-up vs. last private round), DCF as a cross-check, sum-of-the-parts where relevant, then the discount and a range typically about 10-15% wide; post-money and pro forma ownership from the cap table; dilution per S-K Item 506 (net tangible book value per share before/after).

**Follow-on/block pricing.** Discount as a function of size vs. ADTV and market cap, deal type, seller, and recent volatility; for blocks, the bank bids a fixed price (file-to-offer discount) and re-offers overnight; risk is measured by expected re-offer discount and hedging capacity.

**Convertible pricing.** Value = straight bond (issuer credit spread) + call option (volatility, dividend yield, borrow, maturity); coupon and premium trade off along an issuer-indifference curve; conversion price = par / conversion ratio; parity = ratio x share price; premium = conversion price / share price - 1; delta measures equity sensitivity (hedge funds short delta shares); call spreads raise the effective conversion price for the issuer at a cash cost; issuer soft/hard calls, put dates and net-share settlement change the option value. Lattice or Monte Carlo models handle calls/puts.

**SPAC economics.** Cash delivered = trust x (1 - redemptions) + PIPE; effective dilution from promote and warrants (Stanford's Klausner: some targets "may only receive 50% of all the money supplied by investors"); sponsor at-risk capital via private placement; minimum 80% trust-value test for target size.

**Bond math.** Price = sum of CF_t/(1+y/m)^(mt). Macaulay duration = PV-weighted time; modified duration = Macaulay/(1+y/m); DV01 = P x D_mod x 0.0001; convexity = (1/P) d2P/dy2; dP/P = -D_mod dy + 1/2 C dy^2. Spreads: G-spread (to interpolated Treasury), I-spread (to swaps), Z-spread (constant spread over the spot curve), OAS (Z-spread less option cost, computed by lattice/Monte Carlo); for bullet bonds OAS = Z-spread. New-issue fair value = interpolated secondary curve + NIC.

**Make-whole.** Redemption price = max(par, PV of remaining coupons and principal discounted at the reference Treasury yield + make-whole spread). Conventions: IG T+5 to T+15 (AbbVie and Xylem 2026), T+30-50 for HY; worked examples: an Amazon 4.875% 2036 bond with T+15 and 4.47% Treasury gives 103.11 vs. a 97.53 market price; an 8% coupon 7-year bond at T+40 with a 3.8% Treasury gives 122.8. HY call schedules: non-call 2-4 years, then par plus half the coupon stepping to par, plus a 35-40% equity clawback at par plus coupon (practitioner convention).

**Ratings-agency adjusted leverage.** Moody's and S&P start from reported debt and add pensions, hybrids (partial equity credit), AROs, securitizations and, for leases, the reported IFRS 16/ASC 842 liability (S&P haircuts artificially short leases; Fitch instead capitalizes rent at a sector multiple). EBITDA excludes many "run-rate" add-backs that credit agreements allow (AECOM 25% caps; TTM 30%). Outputs: adjusted Debt/EBITDA, FFO/debt, RCF/net debt, coverage, mapped to grids by sector (S&P's financial-risk categories from minimal to highly leveraged). LevFin decks show "as reported," "credit-agreement," and "agency-adjusted" leverage side by side.

**Debt capacity.** Size debt to the binding constraint: max leverage (LMM LBOs 4.0-5.5x total; 2026 recap average 5.04x), minimum interest coverage (2.0x+), FCCR = (EBITDA - cash taxes - maintenance capex)/(cash interest + scheduled principal) with covenant floors ~1.5-2.0x, DSCR >1.0-1.25x, and cushion to any maintenance covenant (25-35%). Stress the base case (revenue, margin, rate shocks) and check liquidity and refinancing walls.

**Sources & uses / LBO returns.** Uses: equity purchase price, refinanced debt, fees and expenses (financing fees 2-3% of debt, M&A fees), minimum cash. Sources: RC draw, TLB, second lien/HY, seller notes, rollover equity, sponsor equity (45-55% of price in 2026 LMM deals). Returns: IRR and MOIC from exit multiple x exit EBITDA less net debt, with sensitivity tables; recaps return capital early (raise DPI) at the cost of higher leverage and lender consent.

**Refinancing NPV.** PV(old debt service) - PV(new debt service + fees + tender premium/make-whole) at the new yield; break-even and years-to-recoup; a public example refinanced 6.00% bonds with 4.625% debt for ~$6.0M annual savings; municipal practice uses 3% of refunded par as an NPV threshold.

**Tender premium analysis.** Fixed-spread price = PV at (reference Treasury yield + fixed spread) to maturity or worst call; premiums to market of 2-5 points (IG callable), 5-15 points (HY), 0-3 points in modified Dutch auctions; early tender premiums (~$30/1,000 within ~10 business days); minimum offer period 20 business days under Rule 14e-1 (five-business-day abbreviated offers for any-and-all IG/non-convertible deals without consents), plus 10 business days after material changes; exit consents, consent fees and waterfalls; compare against make-whole cost and open-market repurchases, subject to MNPI blackout rules.

## 5. Pain points and what is automatable

**Where time goes today.** Assembling comps from PDFs and prospectuses by hand; re-keying bond terms from FWPs into spreadsheets; hunting for the right definition of EBITDA or "Applicable Margin" across 300-page credit agreements; rebuilding market-update slides every week from vendor exports; reconciling three leverage figures (reported, covenant, agency); getting a pricing-vs-range or NIC history for a sector; producing case studies; and answering "please take 15-30 minutes" requests from many coverage teams at once (M&I's description of ECM analyst life). LevFin adds sponsor-driven 3 AM turns of models and commitment papers.

**What AI plus a structured database can do well now.**
1. Parse 424B4/424B5/FWP filings into a normalized deal table (price, spread, greenshoe, lock-up, bookrunners, tranche terms) with citations back to the filing text: entirely from free EDGAR data.
2. Extract and compare document terms (EBITDA add-back caps, incremental baskets, MFN, call schedules, RP builders) from 8-K exhibits and indentures, and diff a new draft against precedents.
3. Generate first drafts of market-update commentary, case-study pages and sales memos from structured data, with numbers linked to sources.
4. Answer bond-math and structuring questions with executable calculators (make-whole, duration, tender price, S&U, LBO returns) rather than free-text estimates.
5. Compute IPO discounts and pricing-vs-range statistics from Ritter-style history plus new filings; flag which comps are stale.
6. Turn a credit agreement into a covenant calendar and headroom monitor once financials are attached.

**Honest limits.**
- **Data that is not free:** real-time order books, syndicate allocations, investor demand, IPT/guidance history, NIC history, loan-level LCD data (flex exercised, OID, secondary quotes), CLO holdings, index OAS by rating (ICE/Bloomberg), full Renaissance stats, Dealogic/LSEG league tables and fee estimates, private-credit loan terms (not filed anywhere, only inferred from BDC filings), and most HY indentures (144A deals appear only when an exchange-offer S-4 is filed).
- **EDGAR coverage gaps:** foreign private issuers on F-forms with different cadence; direct listings; 144A converts; loan repricings done via amendments not always filed; SPAC trust and redemption results scattered across 8-Ks and proxies.
- **Judgment-heavy tasks:** the pricing call, allocations, flex decisions, ratings-agency negotiation, and knowing what "clears the market" remain human and relationship-driven; AI output must be traceable to filings and dated.
- **Accuracy risks:** LLM arithmetic on yields and PVs must run through code; EBITDA definitions differ per document; "leverage" without a definition is meaningless; third-party fee figures online are often wrong (e.g., "1-2% for IG" vs. 0.15-0.75% in 2026 filings).

## 6. Glossary (50 terms)

1. **Gross spread** - underwriting discount (price to public less proceeds to issuer); 7% on mid-size IPOs, ~2% on mega-IPOs, 0.15-0.75% on IG bonds by tenor.
2. **Management fee / underwriting fee / selling concession** - the 20/20/60 split of the gross spread.
3. **Bookrunner (lead-left)** - bank running the book and allocation; **co-manager** - junior syndicate member.
4. **Bookbuilding** - collecting institutional indications at price/size levels during the roadshow to set price.
5. **Price range / file range** - the marketing range on the cover of the preliminary prospectus (red herring).
6. **Priced above/within/below range** - final price relative to the range; a core IPO comp field.
7. **First-day pop** - close-to-offer return on day one; **money left on the table** - pop x shares sold.
8. **IPO discount** - the haircut to trading-comp value at pricing (conventionally 10-15%).
9. **Greenshoe / over-allotment option** - 15% additional shares for 30 days; **syndicate short** and **stabilization** cover the aftermarket.
10. **Lock-up** - 180-day (IPO) or 30-90-day (follow-on) sale restriction on insiders, often with early-release triggers.
11. **Directed share program** - shares reserved at the IPO price for issuer-designated buyers.
12. **Testing-the-waters** - pre-launch investor meetings permitted for EGCs (and, since 2019, all issuers).
13. **EGC** - emerging growth company (JOBS Act) with confidential submission and scaled disclosure.
14. **S-1 / F-1 / 424B4 / 424B5 / FWP / S-3 / WKSI** - registration statement, foreign version, final prospectus, prospectus supplement, free writing prospectus, shelf, well-known seasoned issuer.
15. **Cheap stock** - SEC review issue when pre-IPO option grant values sit far below the IPO range.
16. **Follow-on** - marketed, CMPO (confidentially marketed), overnight/bought deal, block.
17. **File-to-offer discount** - offer price versus price at announcement; **discount to last close** - versus prior close.
18. **ATM (at-the-market)** - dribble-out equity program through a sales agent.
19. **Convertible bond terms** - conversion price/ratio/premium, parity, bond floor, delta, capped call, call spread, net-share settlement.
20. **Dual-track** - parallel IPO and sale process for optionality and negotiating leverage.
21. **SPAC** - blank-check company: trust per unit, promote (founder shares), warrants, redemptions, PIPE, de-SPAC.
22. **IPT / guidance / launch** - the three price announcements in a bond syndication.
23. **New-issue concession (NIC)** - spread pickup over the issuer's secondary curve (3-7 bps in Q1 2026 IG).
24. **Benchmark Treasury / spread to Treasury** - IG pricing convention (e.g., T+50).
25. **G-spread, I-spread, Z-spread, OAS** - spread measures over Treasuries, swaps, the spot curve, and option-adjusted.
26. **Duration / DV01 / convexity** - first- and second-order price sensitivity to yield.
27. **Make-whole call** - redemption at PV of cash flows discounted at Treasury plus a spread (T+5 to T+15 IG; T+50 HY).
28. **Par call** - right to redeem at 100 in the final 1-6 months before maturity (IG) or after the non-call period (HY).
29. **Non-call (NC3/NC4)** - HY call protection; call schedule steps from par plus half coupon to par.
30. **Equity clawback** - HY issuer right to redeem 35-40% of notes with IPO proceeds at par plus coupon.
31. **Change-of-control put** - HY holders' right to sell at 101 on a change of control.
32. **Incurrence covenant** - tested only when the issuer acts (debt incurrence at 2.0x FCCR; restricted payments); **maintenance covenant** - tested quarterly (max leverage, min coverage).
33. **Cov-lite** - loan with only incurrence covenants (over 90% of new BSL); **springing covenant** - revolver-only test above a utilization threshold.
34. **TLA / TLB / RC / second lien / unitranche** - amortizing bank tranche; institutional term loan (1% amortization, 7-year); revolver; junior secured; blended single tranche (private credit) with first-out/last-out AAL.
35. **SOFR floor** - minimum base rate; **OID** - original issue discount (99.0-99.75) converted to yield over a 4-year life.
36. **Market flex / reverse flex** - arranger's right to change price/terms to syndicate; obligation to improve terms if oversubscribed.
37. **Commitment letter / fee letter / ticking fee** - underwriting papers; the confidential economics; fee on undrawn commitments over time.
38. **Underwritten vs. best-efforts vs. club** - arranger guarantees proceeds; arranger sells what it can; small pre-arranged group.
39. **Bank meeting / lender presentation** - the loan roadshow; **CIM** - confidential information memorandum.
40. **Soft call (101 for 6 months)** - repricing protection on TLBs.
41. **MFN (most favored nation)** - incremental debt priced within X bps of existing loans, with a sunset.
42. **Incremental facility / freebie basket** - additional debt capacity (e.g., greater of $490M and 100% of EBITDA).
43. **EBITDA add-backs** - run-rate synergies and cost savings capped at 25-30% of EBITDA in many agreements.
44. **Excess cash flow sweep** - mandatory prepayment (50/25/0% by leverage).
45. **Repricing / amend-and-extend / refinancing** - lowering spread; pushing maturity; replacing debt.
46. **Dividend recapitalization** - new debt funds a distribution to the sponsor; needs solvency opinion and RP capacity.
47. **Leverage stats** - total/senior/net Debt/EBITDA; interest coverage; FCCR; DSCR; agency-adjusted leverage.
48. **LME (liability management exercise)** - tender, exchange, consent solicitation, open-market repurchase; also uptier/drop-down transactions.
49. **Early tender premium / fixed-spread tender / modified Dutch auction** - tender-offer pricing devices; **Rule 14e-1** 20-business-day minimum.
50. **DPI / TVPI / RVPI / dry powder** - sponsor fund metrics tracked by FSG; **CDLI** - Cliffwater Direct Lending Index.
