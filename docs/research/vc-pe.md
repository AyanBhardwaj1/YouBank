Date: 2026-09-20. Scope: VC (analyst to GP; pre-seed to growth; solo GPs, angels, CVC, accelerators, scouts), PE / growth equity (associate to partner; buyouts, growth, secondaries) and fund operations (fund models, LP reporting, portfolio monitoring).

## 1. Role overview and fund economics

### 1.1 The seats

**Venture capital.** The ladder is Analyst, Associate, Senior Associate, Principal/VP, Partner, General/Managing Partner. Compensation and carry rise steeply with seniority (Venture Capital Careers, 2026): Analyst $85–180K base+bonus, no carry; Associate $130–320K, usually no carry; Senior Associate $170–390K, 0–1% of the carry pool if any; Principal/VP $250–750K, 0.5–3% of carry; Partner $400K–1.4M+, 3–12% of carry; Managing/General Partner $550K–2.2M+, 15–30% of carry. Juniors spend most of their time sourcing, screening and supporting diligence; seniors own deal negotiation, board seats, LP relationships and fundraising. At small funds juniors do everything (sourcing, portfolio support, LP materials); at large funds they mainly build the funnel.

Adjacent seats: **solo GPs** and rolling-fund managers (typically $5–20M vehicles, deal-by-deal or quarterly capital), **angels** (self-funded; a common rule is 1–2% of net worth per deal and 5–10% overall, per SuperScout), **scouts** (deploy a firm's capital, paid via a carry pool, deal-by-deal or scout-by-scout arrangements, per AngelList), **corporate VC** (strategic mandates, often with business-unit sign-off and slower cycles), and **accelerators** (YC-style: standard deal, batch cadence, Demo Day as a distribution event).

**Private equity and growth equity.** Analyst/Associate (pre-MBA), Senior Associate, VP, Principal/Director, Partner. M&I puts PE associate total comp at $175–400K in major financial centres and senior associates at $300–550K, with carry only becoming meaningful at VP and above; middle-market hours run 60–70/week, mega-funds are closer to banking hours. Growth equity sits between VC and buyout: minority preferred stakes in revenue-generating companies, little or no debt, sourcing-heavy (cold outbound is a core skill), returns underwritten at 3–5x / 30–40% IRR, and comp roughly 15–20% below buyout PE.

### 1.2 Fund economics (the "2 and 20")

- **Management fee**: 1–3% of committed capital per year, 2% is standard; cumulative fees consume 10–20% of a fund over its life. After the investment period (years 4–5) the fee typically steps down and the base shifts from committed to net invested capital. Board and consulting fees from portfolio companies usually offset the management fee (fee offsets). A $100M fund pays about $20M in fees over ten years and deploys roughly $80M unless fees are recycled.
- **Carried interest**: 20% of profits is the market standard (range 15–30%; premium GPs above 20%). Paid only after LPs get contributed capital back, and, where there is a hurdle/preferred return (usually 8%, standard in buyout, frequently absent in early-stage VC), only after that return is met. A GP catch-up may follow the hurdle. **European (whole-fund) waterfalls** pay carry after all LP capital plus preferred return is returned; **American (deal-by-deal) waterfalls** pay per realised deal and are backed by a **clawback** if the fund overall misses the hurdle.
- **GP commitment**: ~1% of the fund is the norm cited by VC Lab; 1–2% is common, and larger in PE.
- **Term**: 10 years, usually with two one-year extensions; investment period 2–4 years (VC Lab). Capital is called in tranches (20–30% per call is typical), not up front.
- **Recycling**: many LPAs allow early exit proceeds, up to the fee amount or a stated cap, to be reinvested so that 100% of committed capital reaches companies.

### 1.3 Return metrics and where the industry stands

- **DPI** = distributions / paid-in capital (cash actually returned). **RVPI** = residual (unrealised) value / paid-in. **TVPI** = DPI + RVPI. **IRR** is the time-weighted rate; subscription lines can inflate IRR by 200–500 bps without changing cash returns (Pipeline Road).
- VC benchmarks by vintage (Value Add VC, citing Carta Q1 2026 fund performance, Cambridge Associates and PitchBook): 2016 vintage median TVPI ~1.8x with DPI still below 1x; 2018 median TVPI ~1.6x, DPI 0.15x; 2021 median TVPI 1.02x, IRR ~1.4%, DPI near zero at year five. Top quartile for mature vintages is roughly 25%+ net IRR and 3x+ TVPI. Carta data cited in the same research: fewer than half of 2019–2020 vintage funds have returned any capital.
- Buyout benchmarks (Pipeline Road, citing Cambridge, Preqin, Burgiss): 2010–2014 vintages 1.8–2.2x TVPI and 1.4–1.8x DPI; 2019–2021 vintages 1.1–1.4x TVPI and 0.1–0.3x DPI; top-quartile IRR 20%+, bottom quartile 5–8%. LPs expect ~1.5x DPI by year 8 and 1.8–2.0x by end of life.
- The liquidity problem is the defining issue of 2024–2026: Bain's 2026 Global PE report puts distributions at 14% of NAV for the fourth straight year (GFC levels), with 32,000 unsold portfolio companies worth $3.8T, average holds now ~7 years (was 5–6), and the top-to-bottom-quartile return gap at ~14 points for the 2021 vintage.

### 1.4 Fund size, portfolio construction, ownership and reserves

- **Sizes**: 2025 buyout fundraising was $395B (down 16%), total private capital raised $1.3T, with fund closes falling for a fourth year (Bain). On the venture side, firms had raised nearly as much by June 2026 as in all of 2025, but commitments concentrated in established managers; US startups raised more than $400B in H1 2026, mostly AI (PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor Q2 2026). For emerging managers, ~90% of LP commitments go to funds under $15M and a realistic Fund I is $10–12M (Decile Group).
- **Portfolio count**: 20–30 initial investments for a balanced fund; under 15–20 is concentrated, over 40 is index-like (VC Fund Institute; VC Factory). Decile's emerging-manager template: 20–35 positions, ~$250K checks, 3–5% entry ownership diluting to ~2% by exit.
- **Ownership targets**: Michael Kim's heuristic (via VC Factory): about 1% ownership per $10M of fund size at seed, 2% per $10M at pre-seed; lead seed funds historically target 10%+ (some up to 15%); partners at large firms are measured on 15–25% ownership targets (Himalayas JD templates).
- **Reserves**: 30–50% of the fund is typical (range 10–70%+). Decile advises 20–40%, deployed only into the top ~6 positions with a per-company cap, with unspent reserves recycled or returned after year five. VC Factory's worked case: a $40M fund with $0.8M initial checks needs ~$1.9M of follow-on per winner (2.4x the first check) to hold 10% through three rounds, implying ~71% reserves for full defence, which is why most funds accept 40–50% dilution instead.
- **Power law**: the single best company produces ~64% of a fund's return in a Pareto model; a 3x gross fund on $40M therefore needs its winner to exit at ~$1B with 7.5% ownership (VC Factory). The 3x benchmark is the informal "top-tier" bar (VC Lab).
- **Follow-on strategy**: treat reserves as an option, not an obligation; write the deployment rules before the first bridge request; avoid "reserve drift" into defensive bridges of the middle of the portfolio (Decile). Some funds run a 2:1 follow-on ratio; others buy larger ownership up front and reserve 20–40%.

## 2. Day-to-day tasks by seniority, with deliverables and cadence

### 2.1 Venture capital

| Level | Core work | Concrete deliverables and cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst | Inbound screening, market maps, research, model support, LP-update drafting | Triage 200+ inbound decks per quarter within 48h; weekly pipeline stats in CRM; sector maps; cap-table and waterfall models; first drafts of memos (Himalayas JD templates; VC Careers) |
| Associate | Sourcing, first calls, diligence workstreams, memo writing, founder day-to-day contact | 300–800 first-pass opportunities a year (SuperScout); "source 6–8 high-conviction opportunities per quarter"; findings presented within 72h; owns the data room walk-through, revenue reconciliation and customer reference scripts; maintains portfolio dashboards |
| Senior Associate / Principal | Leads deals end-to-end, sector theses, board observer seats, term negotiation | Originates 60–80 qualified opportunities per quarter; memo to IC in under 10 slides; term sheet within ~4 weeks of first meeting; 6–8 board observer/director seats |
| Partner | Sponsors deals at IC, negotiates, sits on boards, raises the fund, LP relations | Leads 8–12 new investments per year; 5–8 boards; quarterly LP letters and annual meeting; fundraising every 2–4 years |
| Managing / General Partner | Strategy, fund economics, fund ops oversight (compliance, valuation, audit) | Owns LPA terms, ILPA reporting, ASC 820 sign-off, auditor and administrator relationships |

**Sourcing.** Four channels: warm intros (portfolio founders are the best-converting source), thesis-driven outbound (build a market map, list every company in the segment, rank by signals, reach out), inbound (website, referrals, accelerators, Demo Days) and events. Signals used to time outreach: headcount and departmental hiring velocity, senior/technical hires, web and app traffic, GitHub activity, product launches (Product Hunt, Show HN), new incorporations and Form D filings, funding and investor interest, founder job changes and stealth departures (Harmonic, Specter, Affinity). Affinity cites that only 3–4% of evaluated deals close and half of dealmakers expect to spend most of their time on sourcing.

**Screening and first calls.** Typical screen: fit with thesis, stage, geography and check size; team; market size and timing; traction; competitive intensity; round dynamics (lead, size, valuation). A first call is 30 minutes: founder story, problem and insight, product demo, traction and cohorts, go-to-market, round details, and a clear next step. Output: CRM note tagged by thesis area, pass/advance decision with a one-line reason (fast, respectful passes are part of brand).

**Diligence workstreams and deliverables.**
- Market: bottom-up TAM, adoption curve, why-now, competitor map, expert calls.
- Product: demo, architecture review, roadmap, security posture, third-party technical review for deep tech.
- Team: 5–10 references including at least two "off-list" (people the founder did not propose), background checks.
- Customers: 5–15 customer/user calls with a structured script (why bought, alternatives, willingness to expand, churn risk).
- Cohort and retention analysis: monthly cohorts of logo, revenue and usage retention; look for stabilisation and improvement in newer cohorts (a16z).
- Unit economics: CAC by channel, payback, gross margin, contribution margin, LTV/CAC, burn multiple.
- Financial model review: rebuild the operating model, reconcile ARR to invoices/bank data, stress churn and margin assumptions.
- Cap table and terms: pro forma fully diluted table, SAFE stack conversion, option pool sizing, existing preferences.
- Legal/IP: charter, prior SAFEs and side letters, IP assignments, employment agreements, litigation, regulatory exposure (the October 2025 NVCA forms add two-way national-security representations under the Outbound Investment Security Program and the Data Security Program, plus QSBS thresholds).

**Investment memo structure** (VC Factory template, consistent with Bessemer's published memos): 1) Executive summary with deal header, thesis (what must be true) and explicit recommendation; 2) Market and why-now, beachhead go-to-market; 3) Team, tied to the specific execution challenges; 4) Product and evidence of need; 5) Business model and unit economics, naming the two or three variables that matter; 6) Traction and cohorts; 7) Competition; 8) Deal terms, ownership, returns analysis and exit scenarios; 9) Risks and mitigants (2–3 material risks with evidence); 10) Post-investment monitoring plan and first-100-day value-add. Strong memos state the risks plainly and explain why underwriting the uncertainty is justified.

**IC.** Weekly partner meeting; memo circulated 24–72h ahead; deal lead presents in 10–15 minutes; decision recorded with conditions (remaining diligence item, milestone tranche, board seat). Bessemer publishes its Shopify, Twitch and Twilio memos, which are the most-read public examples of this format.

**Term sheet negotiation** (market-standard values from Glencoyne and Mayo Law guides to the NVCA term sheet):
- Valuation and price per share: pre- vs post-money; the pool position changes the effective price.
- Option pool: 10–15% post-money pool created in the pre-money (the "option pool shuffle").
- Liquidation preference: 1x non-participating is standard; participating preferred and multiples above 1x are investor-aggressive and rare in early-stage US deals.
- Pro rata: standard rights to maintain ownership; "super pro rata" is off-market.
- Anti-dilution: broad-based weighted average is standard; full ratchet is rare and punitive.
- Board: 3 or 5 seats; a 5-seat Series A board is typically 2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent.
- Protective provisions: consent rights over sale of the company, new senior stock, debt above a threshold, dividends, board size and charter changes.
- Other: drag-along, ROFR/co-sale, information rights, redemption (rare), pay-to-play (down markets), no-shop, and (new in the 2025 NVCA set) milestone-tranched closings.

**Portfolio support.** Monthly or quarterly metric collection; hiring help (executive intros, recruiter access); customer and partner intros; follow-on financing prep; board meetings (quarterly, with a board deck reviewed by the investor in advance); crisis support (bridges, layoffs, sale processes).

**Fund reporting and operations.** Quarterly LP letter (portfolio highlights, new investments, marks, capital account statement); quarterly ASC 820 marks (Level 3 for almost all private positions; methods in section 5.8); capital calls with 10-business-day notice, typically 20–30% of commitments per call in the investment period; annual audit and K-1s; ILPA Reporting Template v2.0 (fees, expenses, offsets, carry, cash-flow detail, internal chargebacks) is required for funds still investing in Q1 2026 or launched after 1 Jan 2026, and the ILPA Performance Template standardises gross and net IRR, TVPI and MOIC with granular or gross-up cash-flow methodologies for funds starting from 1 Jan 2026.

### 2.2 Private equity and growth equity

The deal funnel is more linear than VC: teaser and NDA; CIM review and initial model (associates); indication of interest; management presentation; second-round bid and LOI with exclusivity; confirmatory diligence; financing; SPA; close; 100-day plan; value creation plan; add-ons; exit.

- **Associates**: read 5–15 CIMs a week, build the first-pass LBO and a returns sensitivity, prepare IC screening pages, run diligence trackers, coordinate the quality-of-earnings (QoE) provider, build the lender model and data book, monitor portfolio companies against budget, analyse bolt-on targets and run market research (M&I; CFA Institute).
- **VP / Principal**: run the process, manage advisors (QoE from an accounting firm, legal, commercial diligence from a consultancy, IT/cyber, insurance, tax), negotiate the SPA, lead lender conversations and the debt term sheet, present at IC, sit on portfolio boards.
- **Partner**: sourcing relationships built years ahead of an auction ("pre-auction insight" is Bain's phrase for what wins now), IC votes, LP relations and fundraising, exits.
- **QoE**: a third-party accounting review that produces an adjusted-EBITDA bridge (owner add-backs, one-offs, run-rate adjustments), revenue-quality and customer-concentration analysis, proof of cash, a net working capital peg and a list of debt-like items; it underpins both the purchase price and the lender's leverage case. (Process description from practitioner knowledge; the fetched Investopedia and WSP pages were unavailable.)
- **Lender process**: prepare a lender presentation and model, run a bank/private-credit grid, agree covenants, pricing (SOFR plus spread; all-in cost of debt is 8–9% in Bain's 2026 numbers), and leverage (30–40% of the capital structure today versus ~50% a decade ago).
- **100-day plan** (BowMerge; A Simple Model): drafted during diligence, finalised within two weeks of close; a one-page thesis translation; workstreams (finance/reporting, operations, commercial, technology, human capital, integration) each with an owner and 30/60/90-day milestones; quick wins in the first 30 days; weekly operating reports by day 30, synergy tracking by day 60, LP/IC-ready reporting by day 90. About 90% of PE firms build one (Grant Thornton/PitchBook, via Planr).
- **Value creation**: three levers, EBITDA growth, multiple expansion and deleveraging. Revenue growth accounted for 71% of value created in 2024 exits (Bain via Moonfare); Bain's "12 is the new 5": a 2.5x MOIC over five years now needs 10–12% annual EBITDA growth where 5% used to suffice.
- **Add-on M&A**: add-ons were ~74% of North American PE deal count in 2025 (PitchBook via CohnReznick) and ~76% by Q2 2025 (BowMerge). Each add-on gets its own 100-day plan; leading firms target key-system integration within six months.
- **Exits**: strategic sale, sponsor-to-sponsor, IPO, or a continuation vehicle. Secondary volume rose 41% in 2025 and GP-led continuation vehicles grew 62%, though CVs are still under 10% of exit value (Bain).

### 3.2 Outreach benchmarks and what works

Most published data covers founder-to-VC email, but the mechanics transfer:
- Cold email to investors gets roughly a 1–2% reply rate and ~95% never receive a response; warm introductions convert 20–30% to a first meeting; cold-sourced deals average six months to close versus three for warm (Founders Capital, Substack).
- Elite outreach is short: five to six sentences, under 200 words (Epirus VC), some coaches say under 80; hook the message to a specific signal (a hire, a launch, a milestone) and reference the recipient's actual work; one follow-up 5–7 business days later with new context, Tuesday–Thursday mornings; no attachments on the first touch.
- Portfolio founders are the most under-used intro source; relationship-building should start 5–12 months before a raise, with short monthly updates.

**Anatomy of a great VC-to-founder email.** Subject line that names the specific thing you noticed ("your Show HN on X" / "the ML infra hires"), first line stating why you specifically (thesis, relevant portfolio company, a customer you can introduce), one line of evidence you did the work (a product observation or a data point), an explicit and small ask (20 minutes, or just permission to keep in touch with a quarterly note), and a signature that makes you easy to verify. Avoid generic praise, "love what you're building," and anything that reads as a mass merge.

### 3.3 How operators and angels get equity

- **Advisor shares**: the FAST framework standardises 0.25–1.0% at idea stage, 0.2–0.8% at seed, 0.15–0.6% at Series A+ depending on involvement (standard, strategic, expert), granted as NSOs vesting monthly over two years, usually no cliff (Promise Legal's FAST-style template).
- **Angel checks via SAFEs**: on a post-money SAFE, ownership sold = investment / post-money cap ($500K at a $6.7M cap is ~7.5%, per YC's documents page). Typical 2026 caps: ~$10M post for $250K–1M raises, ~$15M for $1–2.5M, priced rounds above $2.5M (Carta data via Flowjam).
- **Syndicates and SPVs**: a lead invests alongside backers, usually earning 10–20% carry and sometimes a 1–2% fee (SuperScout); AngelList runs formation, filings, K-1s. Prolific leads run hundreds of SPVs (one lead cited at 450 SPVs, 100+ in the past year).
- **Scout programs**: firms allocate budgets to scouts who submit deals through a dashboard and share in a carry pool or deal-by-deal (AngelList Scout Funds).
- **Secondaries**: Hiive shows $300M+ monthly volume, $2B+ live orders and 3,000+ pre-IPO names with hourly pricing; tender offers and secondary sales were the largest source of realised liquidity in Equitybee's data (average 29.4 months to liquidity, 2.6x MOIC on tender/secondary exits). Expect ROFR delays and discounts to the last round.
- **Rolling funds and micro-funds**: quarterly subscription vehicles for operators with deal flow but no LP base.

### 4.2 Marketplace metrics

GMV is not revenue; revenue = GMV x take rate (a16z). Investors ask for GMV growth net of promotions and returns, take-rate trend, contribution margin per transaction, and liquidity: fill rate (share of listings/requests that transact), time to fill, buyer and seller repeat rates, and concentration on each side. a16z's Marketplace 100 ranks private marketplaces by annual GMV using card-panel data; newcomers grew ~1.5x faster than incumbents in its 2023 edition. Rule of thumb, not a measured statistic: take rates cluster around 10–20% for services marketplaces, low single digits for high-ticket or payments-like flows, and 25%+ only where the platform performs the service.

### 4.3 Consumer metrics

DAU/MAU (stickiness), retention curves by cohort (look for the curve flattening), activation, virality (K = invites x conversion), ARPU, paid vs organic CAC. Lenny's Newsletter benchmarks for six-month user retention: consumer social good ~25% / great ~45%; consumer transactional ~30% / ~50%; consumer SaaS ~40% / ~70%; SMB SaaS ~60% / ~80%; enterprise ~70% / ~90%. Downloads and registered users are vanity metrics (a16z).

### 4.4 Fintech metrics

Net revenue after interchange and processing costs, take rate by product (payfac economics of 0.75–1% of volume, card issuing up to ~1.75%, lending as a percentage of loan value tied to who holds risk, per a16z), loss and fraud rates, contribution margin after credit losses, cost of funds, regulatory capital, and revenue per customer (a16z's thesis is that embedding fintech lifts vertical SaaS revenue per customer 2–5x).

### 4.6 Public comps as exit benchmarks

Public SaaS trades broadly at 5–15x revenue, mature software at 15–25x EBITDA, and software buyouts at 12–20x EBITDA (Acquire.com, citing Bessemer, SaaS Capital and SEG). The BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index (EMCLOUD) is the standard forward-revenue-multiple reference for cloud exits; Bessemer's IPO profile is $100M+ LTM revenue, ~65% growth, 120% NRR, 70% gross margin and FCF breakeven visible within 1–2 years. Private ARR multiples by growth cohort: 10–15x at 100%+ growth, 7–10x at 50–100%, 5–8x at 20–50%.

### 4.7 PE metrics

Entry multiples averaged 14.0x EBITDA versus 10.0x a decade ago; leverage is 30–40% of the structure versus ~50% previously; debt costs 8–9%; the standard underwriting case remains 2.5x MOIC / ~20% IRR over five years, now requiring 10–12% EBITDA CAGR; average hold ~7 years; 2025 buyout deal value $904B (+44%), exits $717B (+47%), dry powder $1.3T (Bain 2026). Fund-level: top-quartile buyout IRR 20%+, median 5-year pooled 12–15%.

## 5. Core methodologies

### 5.1 Cap table math and dilution

- **Priced round**: investor % = investment / post-money; post-money = pre-money + new money. A $1M investment at $4M pre is 20% (of $5M post), not 25% (CRV).
- **Post-money SAFE**: ownership = investment / post-money cap, fixed until the priced round; each additional SAFE dilutes founders, not earlier SAFE holders. Conversion price = cap / company capitalisation, where capitalisation includes all SAFEs and notes and the pool (Pulley; YC). ~87% of SAFEs on Carta are post-money.
- **Pre-money SAFE**: conversion price = cap / capitalisation excluding other SAFEs, so SAFE holders dilute one another and final ownership is unknown until the round.
- **Discount SAFE**: price = round price x (1 - discount); cap-and-discount SAFEs convert at the lower of the two. MFN SAFEs inherit later terms.
- **Option pool shuffle** (CRV example): $10M pre, $3M raise, 10% post-money pool. Post = $13M; pool = $1.3M; investor = 23.1%; founders = ($10M - $1.3M)/$13M = 66.9%. At a 20% pool founders fall to ~56.9% while the investor stays at 23.1%. Equivalent share view (Glencoyne): 9M founder shares at a $9M pre implies $1.00/share, but adding 1.2M pool shares pre-money drops the effective price to $0.88.
- **Stacked SAFE conversion**: convert each SAFE at its own price, add the new-money shares and the pool top-up, and solve for the Series A price per share iteratively (the pool and SAFEs both depend on the fully diluted count). Report ownership pre- and post-round for every holder.

### 5.2 Venture capital method

Post-money today = expected exit equity value / target multiple (or / (1+IRR)^years). Required ownership at exit = investment / post-money. Adjust for future dilution with a retention ratio: required ownership today = ownership at exit / (1 - expected dilution). Example: $10M investment, $1B expected exit in 7 years, 10x target, 40% expected dilution: post-money = $100M; ownership at exit needed = 10%; ownership today = 10% / 0.6 = 16.7%, so the investor needs $10M to buy 16.7%, implying a $60M post-money, $50M pre-money. (Standard method; VC Factory's fund-level version works backwards from fund MOIC.)

### 5.3 Comparable rounds

Anchor to Carta/PitchBook medians by stage, sector and geography, then adjust for growth, retention, efficiency, team, round competitiveness and AI premium. Present as a range with the percentile the deal sits in.

### 5.4 DCF for growth companies

Used mainly at growth stage and for ASC 820: five to ten year explicit forecast, growth decay (~30% per year for cloud), margin convergence to public peers, terminal value by exit multiple or Gordon growth, discount rate 20–35% for growth equity reflecting survival risk, and a probability-weighted scenario set instead of a single case.

### 5.5 Exit scenario analysis and returns waterfall

Steps: build the fully diluted table by class; order preferences (senior vs pari passu); for each exit value compute preferred payouts (multiple x invested), decide for each non-participating class whether converting beats taking the preference; allocate the remainder to common and converted preferred pro rata; apply participation caps; deduct management carve-outs and transaction costs; output proceeds and MOIC per holder across a grid of exit values. Glencoyne's example: $30M exit, $5M investment for 25%: non-participating investor takes $7.5M (converts); participating takes $11.25M ($5M plus 25% of the remaining $25M), founders $18.75M instead of $22.5M. Breakpoint charts show where each class switches.

### 5.6 Fund model construction

Inputs: fund size, fees and step-down, GP commit, investment period, pacing (Decile: initial checks over three years, one new position every 5–6 weeks for a solo GP), number of companies, entry ownership, reserve ratio and follow-on rules, expected dilution per round, graduation rates by stage, loss ratio (a common assumption is that roughly half of seed positions return less than 1x and one or two produce most of the return), exit multiples by outcome bucket, timing, recycling. Outputs: gross and net MOIC, TVPI/DPI over time, IRR, J-curve, required winner size, sensitivity to ownership and reserves, and a Monte Carlo of power-law outcomes. Kim's 1%-per-$10M heuristic and VC Factory's "winner must exit at ~$1B" check are the sanity tests LPs apply.

### 5.7 LBO model

Order of build (M&I): transaction assumptions and sources and uses (cash-free, debt-free; sponsor equity as the plug); 2–3 debt tranches with rates, cash vs PIK, mandatory amortisation and an optional cash sweep (50% of available cash flow in the tutorial case); operating model with simple percentage drivers; debt schedule; exit at a multiple on year 4–5 EBITDA less net debt, less management option pool; IRR and MOIC; sensitivity tables on exit multiple and leverage. Add a value-creation bridge (EBITDA growth, multiple change, deleveraging) and, for later work, dividend recaps and management equity waterfalls.

### 5.8 ASC 820 fair value for fund marks

Private positions are Level 3. Methods: calibrate to the last round price and roll forward (backsolve with an option-pricing model to allocate enterprise value across preferred classes); PWERM for companies near an exit; guideline public company and precedent transaction multiples; DCF for cash-generative later-stage companies; and, for very early companies, cost or a common-stock-equivalent allocation. Triggers for change: new rounds, tender offers and secondaries, milestone hits or misses, peer multiple moves, and rights such as ratchets or participation that change allocation. Quarterly valuations with full documentation for the annual audit; auditors prefer independent third-party support (AngelList; Redwood Valuation).

## 7. Glossary

1. **ARR / MRR**: annual/monthly recurring revenue, contracted and repeating. 2. **Anti-dilution (broad-based weighted average)**: conversion-price adjustment on a down round using all outstanding shares in the formula. 3. **Full ratchet**: conversion price reset to the lowest new price; rare. 4. **ASC 820**: US GAAP fair-value standard; Levels 1–3. 5. **Backsolve / OPM**: option-pricing allocation of equity value implied by the latest round. 6. **Burn multiple**: net burn divided by net new ARR. 7. **CAC payback**: months to recover acquisition cost from gross margin. 8. **Capital call**: GP notice requiring LPs to fund part of their commitment. 9. **Carried interest**: GP's share (usually 20%) of fund profits. 10. **Catch-up**: tranche after the hurdle where the GP receives most distributions until it holds its carry share. 11. **Clawback**: LP right to recover carry over-paid under a deal-by-deal waterfall. 12. **CIM**: confidential information memorandum prepared by a sell-side bank. 13. **Continuation vehicle**: GP-led secondary moving assets into a new fund with new LPs. 14. **Cohort analysis**: tracking a signup-period group's retention over time. 15. **DPI / RVPI / TVPI**: distributed, residual and total value to paid-in capital. 16. **Down round**: financing below the prior post-money. 17. **Drag-along**: majority can force minority to join a sale. 18. **Dry powder**: committed but uncalled capital. 19. **EBITDA bridge**: waterfall from current to exit EBITDA by lever. 20. **European vs American waterfall**: whole-fund vs deal-by-deal carry. 21. **Fee offset**: portfolio fees credited against the management fee. 22. **GP commit**: capital the GP invests in its own fund (~1–2%). 23. **GRR / NRR**: gross and net revenue retention over 12 months. 24. **Hurdle / preferred return**: minimum LP return before carry, typically 8%. 25. **IC**: investment committee. 26. **IOI / LOI**: indication of interest; letter of intent with exclusivity. 27. **IRR**: internal rate of return; sensitive to timing and subscription lines. 28. **J-curve**: early negative net returns from fees before markups and exits. 29. **LBO**: acquisition financed largely with debt serviced by the target's cash flows. 30. **Liquidation preference**: preferred holders' right to be paid first (1x non-participating is standard). 31. **Participating preferred**: takes the preference and shares pro rata; often capped. 32. **LPA**: limited partnership agreement. 33. **Magic number**: net new ARR annualised over prior-quarter S&M. 34. **Management fee step-down**: reduction after the investment period, often to a % of invested capital. 35. **MOIC**: multiple on invested capital. 36. **MFN SAFE**: takes the terms of any later SAFE. 37. **Net working capital peg**: agreed normal NWC level in an SPA, with a true-up. 38. **Option pool shuffle**: expanding the pool in the pre-money so existing holders bear the dilution. 39. **Pay-to-play**: investors must fund follow-ons to keep preferred rights. 40. **Post-money / pre-money SAFE**: whether SAFEs are in or out of the capitalisation used to price conversion. 41. **Pro rata right**: right to maintain ownership in future rounds. 42. **Protective provisions**: preferred consent rights on major actions. 43. **PWERM**: probability-weighted expected return method for valuation. 44. **QoE**: quality-of-earnings review producing adjusted EBITDA and NWC analysis. 45. **Recycling**: reinvesting early proceeds within the fund. 46. **Reserves**: capital held for follow-ons. 47. **ROFR / co-sale**: company/investor right to buy shares before a transfer; right to sell alongside. 48. **Rule of 40 / Rule of X**: growth plus margin, or growth weighted ~2x plus margin. 49. **Scout**: individual who sources deals for a firm's capital and shares carry. 50. **Secondary**: sale of existing shares or LP interests. 51. **Sources and uses**: LBO funding table. 52. **Tender offer**: company-sponsored purchase of employee/early shares. 53. **Term sheet**: non-binding summary of financing economics and control. 54. **Tranched financing**: milestone-based closings (formalised in the 2025 NVCA forms). 55. **Value creation plan**: document defining how EBITDA grows during the hold.
