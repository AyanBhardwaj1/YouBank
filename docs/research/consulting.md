**Leverage economics.** Profitability comes from leverage — junior hours billed at multiples of cost under one partner. High-leverage models run 5:1 to 10:1 junior-to-partner ratios. Big 4 graduate intakes were cut 6–30% in 2023–24 (KPMG UK 1,399 → 942) and several observers describe a shift to a "diamond" shape (thinner junior base, stronger mid-level) as AI absorbs junior research and slide work. Clients push back on paying "$500K for reports largely generated by machines," which is the pricing pressure behind outcome-based fees.

## 2. Day-to-day tasks by seniority, with deliverables and cadence

### Analyst / Business Analyst / Associate Consultant (0–3 years)

Owns one workstream's data and slides; ~60–75% of hours are in Excel and PowerPoint. Typical concrete outputs and cadence:

- **Issue tree / hypothesis tree (day 1–3).** Drafts the second and third levels of the tree the EM outlined; each leaf gets an analysis, a data source, an owner and a due date — this becomes the **workplan** (Gantt of analyses mapped to branches). Layers 1–2 are yes/no hypothesis questions ("Can we grow profit to $100M?"), layers 3–4 are open questions answerable with data points.
- **Market sizing (week 1–2).** Builds the **market model**: top-down (industry report → geography/segment filters → penetration) and bottom-up (units × price, or customers × ACV) with a triangulation tab; a divergence beyond ~15% means revisiting filters.
- **Competitive landscape (week 1–3).** Competitor profiles (revenue, growth, segments, pricing, ownership, footprint, recent moves, hiring signals), share estimates via "revenue build-up by competitor" (sum known revenues, estimate the tail), positioning maps.
- **Customer segmentation and pricing analyses.** Cuts transaction data by customer/product/channel; cohort tables; price-realization waterfalls; key purchase criteria (KPC) rankings from survey data.
- **Expert calls and interviews.** Writes the **interview guide** (10–15 questions ordered from open to specific, with probes), schedules through GLG/AlphaSights/Guidepoint/Third Bridge, takes verbatim notes, codes quotes into a synthesis grid (theme × source). On a full CDD, expects to run or listen to dozens of calls; in lower-mid-market CDDs a vendor checklist cites 5–10 current customers, 3–5 lost customers and 3–5 industry experts as minimums.
- **Survey design.** Drafts the questionnaire (screener, KPC importance/performance grid, NPS 0–10, switching intent, willingness-to-pay via Van Westendorp or Gabor-Granger, occasionally conjoint), tests logic, then builds cross-tabs.
- **Benchmarking and cost baselining.** Pulls peer financials (SEC XBRL, CapIQ), computes margin, SG&A %, R&D %, revenue per employee; on ops projects builds the **cost baseline** (GL mapped to cost categories × cost owners, FTE census by function).
- **FDD (associate level).** Ties the trial balance to audited statements and the databook; builds monthly P&L, balance sheet and cash-flow databooks; prepares the **proof of cash** (bank statements ↔ recorded revenue and receipts); pulls support for every seller add-back; builds NWC monthly series and receivables/inventory/payables aging.
- **Turnaround (analyst/associate).** Builds and updates the **13-week cash flow** every week from AR aging, AP ledger, payroll calendar and debt schedules; prepares the budget-vs-actual variance with the top-5 variance explanations; maintains the critical-vendor list and the payment approval log.
- **Economic consulting (analyst).** Cleans transaction/pricing data in Stata/SAS/Python/R, runs regressions and event studies, builds damages spreadsheets, checks every number that goes into an expert report, prepares exhibits.
- **Slides.** Produces 5–15 slides per day: exhibit first, then the action title (≤15 words, two lines max), source line on every page.

**Cadence:** daily team check-in (15 min), EM review of slides most evenings, weekly client working session, "Friday storyline" review, and for DD/turnaround weekly (or twice-weekly) deal-team or lender calls.

### Consultant / Associate / Senior Associate (2–5 years; post-MBA entry)

- Owns a full workstream end to end (e.g., "market and competition" in a CDD; "quality of earnings" in an FDD; "receipts forecast and collections" in a 13WCF).
- Writes the **storyline** for the workstream: ghost deck of action titles in SCQA order (Situation, Complication, Resolution, Proof) before any charts are built; a partner can kill weak logic at this stage — one production vendor estimates ~15% less rework.
- Leads customer-referencing calls and expert calls; synthesizes into "what we heard / so what" pages.
- Runs the pricing, PVM and cost-to-serve analyses; builds the **revenue build** (by customer cohort, product, geography; bottom-up rebuild of the pipeline) and the **scenario model** (base/upside/downside with explicit drivers).
- FDD senior: drafts the adjusted-EBITDA bridge and the NWC peg memo; challenges management on each add-back in the management-interview sessions; prepares net debt and debt-like items schedule.
- Ops: quantifies initiatives (savings, one-time cost, timing, owner), writes initiative charters, designs the **operating model** options (structure, spans and layers, decision rights, processes, KPIs).
- Turnaround: runs the weekly cash meeting agenda (actuals review, payment approvals, collections by owner, two-week liquidity view, actions log), models lender covenant headroom, drafts sections of the IBR.
- **Synergy assessment** (M&A cases): bottom-up by function (procurement, G&A, footprint, sales overlap), phase-in schedule (e.g., 20/50/80/100% over four years), one-time costs to achieve, dis-synergies (customer attrition, cannibalization).
- Cadence: owns the weekly client working-session deck for their stream; prepares the **SteerCo** pages for their workstream.

### Engagement Manager / Project Leader / Manager / Director (5–9 years)

- Frames the problem with the partner and client sponsor; writes the top of the issue tree and the initial hypothesis; owns the **workplan**, staffing and budget burn.
- Reviews every slide; enforces horizontal logic (titles read as a narrative) and vertical logic (each title supported by its exhibit).
- Runs the client relationship day to day; prepares and presents the **weekly progress review** and the **steering committee** deck (agenda: progress vs plan, key findings to date, decisions required, risks, next steps).
- CDD: owns the IC-ready "answer" — market attractiveness, competitive position, target's right to win, base-case revenue build and valuation implications; manages the survey vendor and expert-network budget; joins the PE deal-team calls.
- FDD manager: signs off adjustments, negotiates definitions with the deal team and lawyers (what is "cash-like," what is "debt-like," peg definition, disputed items), drafts the report's key findings and "considerations for the SPA."
- Ops/performance-improvement director: runs the PMO, chairs the weekly initiative review, arbitrates savings claims against the baseline.
- Turnaround director: presents the 13WCF and variance analysis to lenders every week, negotiates forbearance/waivers, controls disbursements; may serve as interim CFO.
- Economic consulting manager/VP: manages the analytic team, coordinates with the expert and counsel, ensures reproducibility of every exhibit.

### Principal / Partner / Managing Director

- Sells and scopes work (proposals, LOEs, fee structures, success-fee negotiation); owns the P&L of the client.
- Sets the hypothesis on day one, reviews the ghost deck weekly, and personally delivers the SteerCo and final readout.
- CDD partner: takes the "would I invest?" call, speaks to the IC, signs the report's reliance letter (non-reliance for third parties).
- FDD partner: signs the report, handles the escalation calls with the seller's advisers and with the buyer's lenders (independence is required for lender underwriting).
- Restructuring MD: appointed **CRO** — "a senior officer of a company given broad powers to renegotiate all aspects of a company's finances," reporting to the board yet with executive authority (a CRO can replace a CEO, as at Hostess Brands in 2012); negotiates with secured lenders, bondholders, unsecured creditors' committees; testifies in bankruptcy court.
- Economic consulting: the testifying expert (often an academic) plus the firm's senior officers who manage the matter.

**Fields that matter in a market model:** market definition and boundaries; unit volume and price by segment (product × customer type × geography × channel); penetration/adoption rate; growth drivers with elasticities; share by competitor with a stated method (reported, triangulated, estimated); profit pool by value-chain step (revenue and operating margin per step); scenario toggles (base/upside/downside) with drivers exposed; source and confidence tag on every input.

**Fields that matter in a competitor profile:** ownership and funding; revenue, growth and EBITDA margin (reported or estimated with method); segment and geographic mix; headcount and hiring trend; product/portfolio and pricing (list vs pocket); go-to-market model and channel partners; key customers and concentration; recent M&A, capex, leadership changes; NPS/reviews; stated strategy; strengths/weaknesses versus KPCs; threat level to the target.

## 4. Core methodologies in detail

**MECE issue trees and hypothesis trees.** Decompose the question until each leaf is testable. Four math-tree techniques: proven formulas (Profit = Revenue − Cost), dimensional analysis (finding the mathematical complement), funnels (sequential stages), sum of segments (BUs, regions). Five "ways to be MECE" layered iteratively: algebra, process, segmentation, conceptual frameworks, opposite pairs. Problem ("why") trees diagnose; solution ("how") trees prescribe. Principles: separate fundamentally different problems early, one layer at a time, MECE at every level, prioritize insight over technical purity, make branches falsifiable. The tree drives the workplan: each leaf → analysis → data source → owner → due date.

**Market sizing math.** Top-down: reliable macro figure → market boundary → segment → eligible customers (SAM) → adoption (SOM) → revenue. Worked example: $50B global analytics software TAM → $10B SAM (North America mid-market, 20%) → 10% capture = $1B SOM → ÷ $50k ACV ≈ 20,000 customers; sensitivity 5%/$40k → $0.5B, 15%/$60k → $2.25B. Bottom-up: reachable accounts × close rate × ACV (500 × 40% × $50k = $10M year one). Triangulate: if top-down and bottom-up agree within ~15% the assumptions are probably sound; new products typically capture 0.1–2% of SAM early. Always record sources and confidence, and expose pessimistic/base/optimistic cases.

**Porter's five forces.** Rivalry, buyer power, supplier power, threat of entry, threat of substitutes. Porter's warning: managers "define competition too narrowly, as if it occurred only among today's direct competitors." In CDD this becomes the "market attractiveness" section: entry barriers, switching costs, concentration on each side, substitute economics, and the resulting sustainable margin.

**Profit pools (Bain, Gadiesh & Gilbert 1998).** Four steps: define the pool's boundaries (value-chain activities), estimate the pool's total size, estimate each activity's size, check and reconcile. Plot revenue on the x-axis and operating margin on the y-axis; the area is profit. Data rarely maps cleanly to activities, so estimates are built from segment disclosures, pure-play comparables and cost build-ups, then reconciled to the total.

**Customer advocacy: NPS and referencing.** NPS = % promoters (9–10) − % detractors (0–6) on a 0–10 recommend question; relationship NPS (overall) vs transactional NPS (after an interaction). Bain positions it as a system: pair the score with behaviour (repeat purchase, referrals) and with verbatims. In CDD, an NPS below ~20 is a red flag *(vendor checklist)*; independent (not seller-supplied) customer referencing is the norm, with lost-customer calls to understand churn and win/loss.

**Cohort analysis and revenue quality.** Group customers by acquisition period; track logo retention, gross revenue retention and net revenue retention (NRR) by cohort; compute LTV:CAC (≥3:1 healthy) and CAC payback (<12–18 months); flag top-3 customers ≥50% of revenue, NRR <100% and pipeline coverage <3× quota. Rebuild the pipeline bottom-up by customer/cohort to validate the forecast.

**Price-volume-mix (PVM).** Sequence: price effect = Δ average selling price × prior-period volume; volume effect = Δ volume × current price − mix impact; mix effect = mix-weighted volume differential × price differential to the base average price. FTI's worked example: revenue +20.1% ($86.6M) decomposed into price +3.5% ($15M), volume +10.4% ($44.6M), mix +6.3% ($27M); a 5% price increase on smartphones caused a −12.5% volume decline (negative mix). Rules: compute at SKU level (group-level analysis hides mix inside price), keep units consistent, filter zero-volume periods and new/discontinued SKUs into separate bridge bars, use pocket price net of discounts/rebates/freight. Buyers apply a "durability test" to each bar (contractual? recurring? did customers churn after the increase?).

**Cost-to-serve.** Activity-based costing applied to customers/channels: assign order handling, delivery, returns, support, sales effort and terms (DSO, discounts) to each customer; produce a whale curve of cumulative profit by customer; drives pricing, minimum-order and channel decisions. *(practice convention)*

**Zero-based budgeting.** Build deep visibility into cost drivers (GL lines × cost categories × cost-category owners), set targets from the bottom up rather than last year's base, govern with monthly owner reviews. McKinsey reports 10–25% SG&A reductions often within six months; a European utility found $150M from a ~$900M baseline; an Accenture survey found 91% of ZBB programmes met or exceeded targets. Variants: zero-based redesign (Bain), zero-based productivity/marketing (McKinsey), zero-based supply chain (Accenture).

**Peer benchmarking from SEC data.** Using XBRL company facts (tags such as `Revenues`/`RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`, `CostOfRevenue`, `SellingGeneralAndAdministrativeExpense`, `ResearchAndDevelopmentExpense`, `OperatingIncomeLoss`, `AccountsReceivableNetCurrent`, `InventoryNet`, `AccountsPayableCurrent`), compute gross margin, EBITDA/EBIT margin, SG&A %, R&D %, DSO/DIO/DPO and cash conversion cycle; revenue per employee needs headcount parsed from 10-K Item 1 (not reliably in XBRL). Caveats: extension tags and custom taxonomies (e.g., Exxon) leave gaps; calendarization for non-December fiscal years; segment data lives in notes. Present as quartiles with the target's position and the gap-to-median in dollars.

**QoE adjustment taxonomy.** (1) Seller's adjustments — claimed add-backs (non-recurring expenses, excess owner compensation, personal expenses, litigation, one-time projects); providers commonly disallow 10–30% of proposed add-backs, especially "one-offs" that recur every year. (2) Diligence adjustments — corrections the provider finds (accounting errors, unrecorded liabilities, revenue cut-off, capitalized costs that are really opex, related-party terms restated to arm's length). (3) Pro forma / run-rate adjustments — normalizing TTM for known changes (new rent, new contracts, price increases, acquisitions, lost customers). Supporting analyses: proof of cash (bank statements ↔ recorded revenue/receipts), revenue recognition testing, customer/product concentration at gross-profit level, related-party review, run-rate studies. Price impact is mechanical: at 6× EBITDA, $200k of rejected add-backs removes $1.2M of enterprise value. Baker Tilly's cash-earnings lens: FCF = EBITDA ± ΔNWC − capex − taxes.

**NWC peg methodology.** Define NWC per the SPA (usually trade receivables + inventory + prepaids − trade payables − accrued expenses, excluding cash, debt, income taxes and deal-related items); build 24–36 months of monthly NWC; set the peg as a trailing-twelve-month average to smooth seasonality (or a seasonally matched average when closing month is known); adjust for aged receivables, obsolete inventory, stretched payables (sellers "artificially inflate the peg" by extending payables pre-sale), non-recurring balances; agree the definition and illustrative calculation in the SPA; true-up 60–90 days post-close. Sellers "frequently lose more money at the working capital true-up than through earnings adjustments" — in one worked example the peg cost $1.4M vs a smaller EBITDA adjustment.

**Net debt and debt-like items.** Beyond bank debt: deferred revenue (undelivered service), accrued but unpaid bonuses, capital/finance leases, unfunded pensions, unpaid sales/payroll taxes, deferred consideration and earn-outs from prior acquisitions, customer deposits, unusual accruals, accrued interest, rent-free period accruals, debt break costs; in the same example these added $760k to net debt.

**Synergy assessment.** Revenue synergies = synergy % × combined revenue × realization %, then margin- and tax-affected; cost synergies = synergy % × combined cost base × realization %; phase in (e.g., 20/50/80/100% over years 1–4); subtract one-time costs to achieve; add dis-synergies (customer attrition, cannibalization, supplier loss, tax leakage). Cost synergies are far more bankable than revenue synergies; practitioner guides credit only a fraction of announced synergies. Benchmark with historical deals in the sector, bottom-up by function, and market ratios such as SG&A % of revenue.

**Scenario modeling.** Driver-based three-statement or EBITDA-bridge model with named scenarios (base/upside/downside; management case vs sponsor case vs lender case), each driver sourced to an analysis in the tree; tornado sensitivities on the five biggest drivers; for turnarounds, weekly liquidity scenarios (base, stress, stress + no revolver) against the minimum-cash policy.

**13-week cash flow.** Receipts (customer collections by top account, card settlements, refunds, asset sales); disbursements (payroll, taxes/benefits, rent, critical vendors, AP, debt service, capex, professional fees); financing (revolver draws/repays, DIP funding); summary (beginning cash, net change, ending cash, minimum cash policy, headroom, revolver availability). Build steps: lock the cash truth (all accounts, restricted cash), load week-zero actuals, drive receipts from invoicing and collection actions, control disbursements with approval rules, add covenant visibility. Weekly: actual-vs-forecast with top-5 variances, action log with owners, one-page decisions summary. "If there is no weekly meeting with approvals, variances, and owners, the forecast will not change outcomes."

## 5. Pain points, and what AI + data can honestly automate

**Where the hours go.** Analysts spend most of their time (a) finding and cleaning data (GL exports, data-room spreadsheets, filings), (b) rebuilding the same benchmark and market-model skeletons case after case, (c) transcribing and coding interviews, (d) producing and reformatting slides, (e) tying numbers across deck, model and databook, and (f) waiting on data-room requests. FDD teams move 400–500 documents across three request waves; CDD teams book and sit through dozens of calls in two to three weeks; turnaround teams rebuild the 13WCF every week from raw ledgers.

**What the firms already automate.** McKinsey's Lilli (used by ~72% of 45,000 staff, ~500k queries/month) searches 100k+ internal documents and drafts slides; BCG's Deckster polishes decks; Bain's Sage, Deloitte's PairD, KPMG's KymChat and EYQ are analogous. Reported gains: ~30% research-time savings at McKinsey; vendors claim AI now does "roughly 80%" of junior research and slide-generation work, with output needing 15–20% human revision — treat these as upper-bound marketing figures. Expert networks now expose transcript libraries through APIs and MCP (Guidepoint, Third Bridge), which shifts the first pass from live calls to transcript synthesis.

**Automatable with high confidence (YouBank scope):**
- Pulling and normalizing peer financials from SEC XBRL, computing margin/SG&A/R&D/working-capital benchmarks with quartiles and footnoted tags.
- First-draft competitor profiles from filings, websites and news, with every field sourced.
- Top-down market-size triangulation from cited public statistics (Census establishment counts × BLS wage/BEA output data) with an audit trail.
- PVM, cohort/NRR, concentration and NWC-seasonality computations from uploaded transaction or GL data, plus draft commentary.
- Proof-of-cash and trial-balance tie-outs, add-back support matching, recurrence checks ("this 'one-off' appears in 3 of 4 years").
- Interview/transcript coding into a theme × source grid with quotes; survey cross-tabs and KPC gap charts.
- Ghost-deck generation (action titles in SCQA order) from an issue tree and findings log; slide-to-model consistency checks.
- 13WCF variance narratives and lender-report drafting from the weekly actuals.
- Restructuring precedent searches (8-K restructuring charges, Chapter 11 dockets) and synergy benchmarks from announced deals.

**Not automatable, or only assistable:** the "answer" and its judgment calls; live customer/expert conversations and the trust that yields candid answers; negotiating adjustments with the counterparty; management-claim validation that requires site visits and source documents; court testimony; anything requiring MNPI-safe compliance judgement; private-company data that simply is not public (AI cannot conjure a private competitor's margin — it can only triangulate with stated confidence). The honest positioning is "hours-to-minutes on the reproducible 60%," not "replace the team."

**Adoption constraints to design for:** confidentiality (deal codenames, no training on client data, per-engagement data isolation), auditability (every number traceable to a source and formula), Excel/PowerPoint as the system of record (export must be native and editable), and partner-level trust (visible confidence levels, no silent estimates).

## 6. Glossary (48 terms)

1. **Action title** — full-sentence slide headline stating the takeaway (≤15 words).
2. **Add-back** — expense added back to reported EBITDA as non-recurring or non-operating.
3. **Adjusted (normalized) EBITDA** — EBITDA after seller, diligence and pro forma adjustments.
4. **Bottom-up sizing** — market size built from units × price or accounts × ACV.
5. **Cash-free, debt-free** — deal basis where cash and debt are settled by the seller; equity value = EV − net debt ± NWC adjustment.
6. **CDD (commercial due diligence)** — independent test of market, competitive position, customers and revenue plan.
7. **Completion accounts** — post-closing price adjustment mechanism using closing balance sheet (vs locked box).
8. **Cohort analysis** — tracking customer groups by acquisition period for retention and revenue expansion.
9. **Cost baseline** — mapped, validated starting cost base against which savings are measured.
10. **Cost-to-serve** — activity-based cost of serving a customer/channel.
11. **CRO (chief restructuring officer)** — officer with broad powers to renegotiate a distressed company's finances.
12. **Databook** — Excel workbook of monthly financials and analyses underpinning an FDD report.
13. **Debt-like item** — obligation treated as debt in the equity bridge (deferred revenue, accrued bonuses, leases, earn-outs).
14. **DIP financing** — debtor-in-possession loan in Chapter 11; lenders require a 13-week budget.
15. **Dis-synergy** — value lost in a merger (customer attrition, cannibalization).
16. **EBITDA bridge** — waterfall from reported to adjusted EBITDA, or from current to target EBITDA by initiative.
17. **Engagement manager (EM) / project leader (PL)** — day-to-day case leader.
18. **Equity bridge** — enterprise value to equity value: minus net debt and debt-like items, plus cash-like items, ± NWC adjustment.
19. **Expert network** — marketplace for paid one-hour calls with industry practitioners (GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint, Third Bridge).
20. **FDD (financial due diligence)** — QoE, NWC, net debt and cash-flow workstreams supporting a transaction.
21. **Ghost deck** — outline of action titles only, built before exhibits.
22. **Gross/net revenue retention (GRR/NRR)** — revenue retained from a cohort excluding/including expansion.
23. **Hypothesis-driven** — start with an answer and design analyses to prove or disprove it.
24. **IBR (independent business review)** — lender-commissioned review of a distressed borrower's plan and liquidity.
25. **IC memo** — investment committee memorandum; CDD/FDD sections feed it.
26. **Issue tree** — MECE decomposition of the question into sub-questions.
27. **KPC (key purchase criteria)** — factors customers weigh when choosing suppliers; used in importance/performance grids.
28. **Locked box** — fixed price at a historical balance sheet date with leakage protections (no completion accounts).
29. **LTV:CAC** — customer lifetime value to acquisition cost; ≥3:1 is a common threshold.
30. **MECE** — mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive.
31. **Net debt** — debt and debt-like items less cash and cash-like items at closing.
32. **NPS** — net promoter score, % promoters minus % detractors.
33. **NWC peg** — target normal working capital in the SPA; closing NWC above/below it adjusts price.
34. **One-time costs to achieve** — integration/restructuring costs required to deliver synergies.
35. **Operating model** — structure, processes, governance, decision rights and KPIs through which strategy is executed.
36. **Pocket price** — realized price after discounts, rebates, freight and terms.
37. **Profit pool** — total profit across an industry's value chain, mapped by activity.
38. **Proof of cash** — reconciliation of recorded revenue/receipts to bank statements.
39. **PVM (price-volume-mix)** — decomposition of revenue or margin change into price, volume and mix effects.
40. **QoE (quality of earnings)** — analysis of the sustainability and cash conversion of earnings.
41. **Red-flag CDD** — short, pre-LOI scan for deal-breakers.
42. **Run-rate** — annualized current performance after known changes.
43. **SCQA** — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer storyline structure.
44. **SteerCo** — steering committee of client executives that reviews progress and takes decisions.
45. **TAM / SAM / SOM** — total, serviceable and obtainable market.
46. **13-week cash flow (13WCF)** — weekly receipts/disbursements/financing forecast for liquidity control.
47. **Value creation plan (VCP)** — hold-period plan of EBITDA-moving initiatives; the 100-day plan is its first chapter.
48. **Workplan** — analyses mapped to owners, sources and dates, derived from the issue tree.
