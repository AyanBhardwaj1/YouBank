## 1. SEC (US): AI rule, AI-washing, Marketing Rule, recordkeeping, Form PF, Reg S-P, 2026 exam priorities

### Takeaway
There is no AI-specific SEC rule: the predictive data analytics proposal was withdrawn in June 2025. AI is now policed through existing law: the antifraud provisions and Marketing Rule (AI-washing), Rule 204-2 recordkeeping, the compliance rule, and exams. The FY2026 exam priorities name AI as a cross-cutting risk and say examiners will test (a) whether a firm's AI claims are accurate and (b) whether it has governance, controls and human oversight over its AI use. Firms therefore need evidence that their AI use is controlled, rather than a checklist against a new rule. Reg S-P incident-response duties are now live for all advisers. Form PF has slipped again to 1 July 2027. A deregulatory rewrite of electronic-communications recordkeeping is on the agenda but has not been proposed.

### Cited Findings
**Predictive data analytics (PDA) rule: WITHDRAWN**
- The PDA/conflicts proposal (July 2023) would have required broker-dealers and advisers to "eliminate or neutralize" conflicts from predictive technologies. The SEC under Chair Paul Atkins withdrew it as one of 14 Gensler-era proposals. Reports date the announcement 12 June 2025, and the Federal Register notice is dated 17 June 2025. The SEC said it does not intend to issue final rules on these proposals. The same batch also withdrew the safeguarding/custody, ESG disclosure, adviser outsourcing and adviser cybersecurity risk-management proposals. — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/06/sec-clears-unfinished-rulemakings-from-regulatory-agenda); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-formally-withdraws-fourteen-rule-proposals)
- Commentators expect the SEC to keep addressing AI through staff statements, risk alerts and exams rather than a rule. — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/sec-withdraws-ai-and-esg-proposed-rules-from-gensler-s-tenure)

### Inferences
- With no AI rule, a firm's defence in an exam is its own written AI policy plus evidence that it followed it. That evidence includes logs of what the AI produced, who reviewed it, and how AI claims in marketing tie back to actual use. A tamper-evident AI ledger with reviewer sign-off and an exportable governance report fits this exam question directly. Pitch it as "exam-ready evidence for the 2026 priorities' AI-governance and AI-washing tests", not as compliance with an AI rule.
- A Marketing Rule / AI-washing check on LP letters fits the two live enforcement themes: substantiation of AI claims and performance presentation. The product should show the substantiation trail, not just flag words.
- The pending 204-2 e-comms modernization (deregulatory) could narrow what must be retained. Exams still treat recordkeeping as central, though, and AI prompts and outputs that are "business communications" are a grey zone. Selling "keep the substantive research record, provably unaltered" is robust to either outcome.
- Reg S-P vendor-oversight duties mean managers will ask any AI research vendor for 72-hour breach notice, data-handling terms and SOC-type evidence. That is a sales prerequisite, not a differentiator.
- A FY2027 exam priorities release is plausible in mid-November 2026 (FY2026 came out 17 Nov 2025); watch for it.

- Could not confirm whether the 204-2 electronic-communications proposal was issued in Oct–Nov 2026.
- No Marketing Rule risk alert specific to AI was confirmed.

## 2. EU AI Act, ESMA, FCA, DORA

### Takeaway
For a fund manager using generative AI for research, the EU AI Act's binding duties are mainly AI literacy (since Feb 2025) and the Article 50 transparency rules (Aug 2026). Most fund-research use cases are not Annex III high-risk; credit scoring is. Annex III high-risk obligations were delayed by the Digital Omnibus to 2 Dec 2027. ESMA's May 2024 statement already expects MiFID firms to keep AI records and to hold the management body accountable. The FCA is pushing live testing and a good- and poor-practice report rather than new rules. DORA (from 17 Jan 2025) applies ICT third-party-risk duties that will bite on AI vendors.

### Cited Findings
- The **Digital Omnibus on AI** is FINAL. Parliament endorsed it on 16 Jun 2026 and the Council adopted it on 29 Jun 2026. It was signed on 8 Jul 2026 and entered into force on 27 Jul 2026, reportedly as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Annex III stand-alone high-risk obligations moved from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, and Annex I product-embedded systems to **2 Aug 2028**. Article 50 transparency obligations still apply from **2 Aug 2026**, with a watermarking grace period to 2 Dec 2026. — [Sidley, 22 Jun 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-agrees-digital-omnibus-deal-simplify-ai-rules); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/). Sources disagree on the Official Journal publication date (some say 24 Jul).
- **ESMA public statement (30 May 2024, ESMA35-335435667-5924), GUIDANCE under MiFID II:**
  - The management body remains responsible for decisions "irrespective of whether those decisions are taken by people or AI-based tools", and the board should understand how AI is applied.
  - Firms are expected to keep comprehensive records on AI use, including decision-making processes, data sources, algorithm changes and complaints.
  - Third-party AI is subject to the MiFID outsourcing rules.
  - The statement is without prejudice to the AI Act and DORA.
  - Sources: [ESMA PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf); [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/clymordyh0020txdsa6abcze8/esma-published-guidance-for-firms-using-ai-in-investment-services); [Dillon Eustace](https://www.dilloneustace.com/insights/esma-issues-guidance-on-the-use-of-ai)
- **FCA:**
  - AI Live Testing (with technical partner Advai). The second cohort was announced 21 Apr 2026 with 8 firms, including Barclays, Experian, Lloyds and UBS; use cases include targeted investment support. Testing ends at end-2026, with an evaluation report due Q1 2027.
  - The FCA will publish a good- and poor-practice report on AI in financial services later in 2026.
  - The Supercharged Sandbox runs in parallel.
  - Sources: [Mondo Visione / FCA](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/uk-financial-conduct-authority-announces-second-cohort-for-ai-live-testing-2026421/); [FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-set-launch-live-ai-testing-service); [FStech](https://www.fstech.co.uk/fst/FCA_Expands_AI_Testing_With_Major_Banks_And_FinTechs.php)
- The Dutch AFM published a report titled "AI in the Dutch asset management sector: use is growing, and so are the risks". — [Citi GTFS Bite-Sized 2026](https://www.citibank.com/icg/sa/flippingbook/2026/Global-Trustee-and-Fiduciary-Services-Bite-Sized-Issue-5-2026/)

### Inferences
- An EU/UK manager's binding AI duty today is light, but ESMA's "comprehensive records on AI use" and board-accountability expectations map almost one-to-one onto a hash-chained ledger plus a governance report for the board/management body. The pitch: "show your board and your NCA what the AI did and who signed off."
- DORA ICT third-party registers and contractual requirements mean EU AIFMs/UCITS ManCos that buy an AI research tool will need register entries, exit plans and incident reporting from the vendor. This is again a sales prerequisite.
- The AI Act delay reduces regulatory urgency in the EU. Demand in 2026–27 is therefore more likely to come from allocator ODD than from the AI Act itself, which is an inference.

### Gaps
- DORA's application date (17 Jan 2025) and its scope covering AIFMs/UCITS ManCos come from background knowledge. They were not confirmed by a fetched source in this session; verify against Regulation (EU) 2022/2554.
- No 2025–2026 ESMA follow-up on AI in investment management was found. The FCA good- and poor-practice report had not been located, so whether it is published is unknown.
- No source found that maps fund-manager research use cases to AI Act risk tiers.

## 3. What allocators ask about AI in ODD; ILPA, AIMA and SBAI guidance

### Takeaway
Allocator ODD has moved from "do you use AI?" to "show us your AI governance": a written policy, where AI is used, human oversight and audit trails. Questions are mostly bolted onto existing DDQs (AIMA, ILPA) or come from separate Gen-AI DDQs on platforms such as DiligenceVault. No ILPA AI module was found. This is the strongest demand signal for an "AI DDQ answered from the record" product.

### Cited Findings
- AIMA research (16 Sep 2025, about 150 managers): 95% of hedge funds use generative AI in some capacity, up from 86% in 2023. Nine in ten investors think Gen AI will improve hedge fund performance within three years, and 60% are more likely to allocate to managers investing meaningfully in Gen AI. That investor sample is small: Opalesque reportedly cites an 18-investor group. — [Marex summary](https://marex.com/news/2025/12/generative-ai-in-hedge-funds-from-experimentation-to-everyday-use); [AIMA press release](https://www.aima.org/article/press-release-front-office-gen-ai-adoption-shifts-from-if-to-when-for-leading-fund-managers-aima-research-finds.html)

### Inferences
- The recurring allocator asks are a written AI policy, an inventory of where AI is used, human-in-the-loop evidence and audit trails. Each maps to an existing YouBank artifact: the governance report, the ledger, reviews and process-health metrics. The product gap is turning these into DDQ-ready answers with attached evidence. "Answers drafted from the record", meaning substantiated rather than boilerplate, is differentiated against generic RFP/DDQ automation tools such as AutoRFP, Inventive and Arphie, which draft from a content library.
- Allocators could buy a read-only "verification view" of a manager's ledger (hash proofs and the decision journal) as part of ODD. A two-sided play: managers pay to produce the evidence, allocators pay or require it for monitoring. Willingness to pay on the allocator side is unproven.

## 4. Emerging manager market: launches, seeding, what helps a small manager raise

### Takeaway
Launch activity is at multi-year highs and allocators are funding emerging managers. The barriers remain cost ($1–2M to start) and the roughly three-year track-record wait, so tools that make a small manager look "institutional" and make its track record and process verifiable from day one are directly relevant.

### Cited Findings
- HFR: 562 hedge fund launches in 2025, the most since 2021; closures fell to 287, a 20-year low. — [HedgeCo, Apr 2026](https://hedgeco.net/news/04/2026/hedge-fund-launches-surge-to-a-four-year-high-as-industry-assets-hit-record-5-16-trillion.html); [Pulse/Hedgeweek](https://www.pulse.bot/hedge-funds/news/hedge-fund-launches-hit-four-year-high-as-investors-position-for-volatility-954a248f-c3ec-4cfc-abbe-c927450863e4/)
- Q1 2026: launches and liquidations both rose sharply, and industry capital reached about $5.22tn. — [Opalesque, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.opalesque.com/715450/Hedge_fund_launches_and_liquidations_both_jump_to545.html)
- Two-thirds of allocators committed capital to emerging managers in 2025 and plan to again in 2026. — [Hedgeweek Ones to Watch 2026](https://resources.hedgeweek.com/ones-to-watch-2026)
- Seeding: "The Seed Fund" joint venture is investing $25–50M per portfolio in emerging hedge fund managers (Opalesque, Jul 2026). Tages Emerging Opportunities II reported a first close of more than $200M. — [Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/56111/Seed_space_still_small_but_emerging111.html); [Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/industry-updates/5328/tages-capital-raises-over-in-capital-commitments.html)
- Start-up costs are $1–2M, and managers typically need a three-year track record before institutional capital arrives (Hedge Fund Journal, Feb 2026, via search summary). — [Opalesque, Feb 2026](https://www.opalesque.com/637753/Angel_or_devil_Seeding_arrangements_for_emerging775.html)

### Inferences
- A server-timestamped decision journal that cannot be backdated, combined with forecast calibration, gives an emerging manager verifiable process evidence before the three-year track record exists. Seeders and ODD teams could use it to underwrite process instead of only returns. This is the clearest wedge for small managers: cheaper than adding compliance or IR headcount within a $1–2M launch budget.
- Willingness to pay is likely to be modest per seat (small-firm budgets) but high in urgency around a raise.

### Gaps
- No H1 2026 HFR launch count was found. No survey quantifies how much verified process evidence shortens fundraising. No pricing data exists on what emerging managers spend on IR or compliance tooling.

## 5. Who sells compliance and ODD tooling, at what prices, and the gaps

### Takeaway
Spending is real and rising. Archiving vendors now capture Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude prompts. Hadrius raised $27M in July 2026 for agentic compliance. ODD platforms (DiligenceVault, Dasseti) are adding AI. Pricing is mostly quote-based. The gap: archivers capture AI conversations, compliance suites manage policies, and ODD platforms move questionnaires, but none ties research outputs, human review, decisions and investor claims into one verifiable chain.

### Inferences
- **Willingness to pay** is shown by venture funding (Hadrius $27M), incumbents building AI-capture SKUs for Copilot, ChatGPT and Claude, and Global Relay's reported 3,000% jump in ChatGPT capture. Managers are already paying to record AI use. The next purchase is proving that the AI use was governed: review, decision linkage, and substantiation of marketing claims.
- **Positioning against incumbents:**
  - YouBank should complement, not replace, the archivers. Exporting ledger entries into Smarsh or Global Relay, or proving chain integrity alongside them, removes the "we already have an archive" objection.
  - Against Hadrius and ComplySci-type suites, the differentiation is that the evidence comes from the research workflow itself, not attestations.
  - Against DiligenceVault and Dasseti, YouBank can be the manager-side evidence source those platforms pull answers from (integration or partnership).
- **Pricing anchors:** archiving starts at about $75–100/month per firm plus seats. Compliance suites cost hundreds to low thousands per month. Enterprise ODD platforms are quote-based. A governance-evidence add-on for small and mid managers plausibly sits in the low thousands per year. This is an inference and has not been validated.

### Gaps
- No public pricing was found for Global Relay, ComplySci, Saifr, Backstop, DiligenceVault or Dasseti.
- Saifr and ComplySci did not appear in results, so there is no current data on their 2026 products or prices.
- No vendor was found that offers hash-chained, tamper-evident AI research ledgers specifically. This suggests a gap, but absence of evidence is not proof.
